## vol-managed

### Moreira, A. & Muir, T. (2017), "Volatility-Managed Portfolios", Journal of Finance 72(4), 1611-1644
- 方法：Managed factor f_sigma_{t+1} = (c / sigma_hat_t^2) * f_{t+1} (Eq. 1), where sigma_hat_t^2 = RV_t^2 = previous month's realized variance of DAILY returns (Eq. 2: sum over ~22 days of squared demeaned daily returns). c is a constant setting the managed portfolio's unconditional vol equal to buy-and-hold (c does not affect Sharpe). Monthly rebalancing; alphas decline as the rebalancing period lengthens. No parameter estimation needed in real time. Variants tested (Table V): scale by 1/RV (vol not variance), by AR(1)-expected variance, and CAPPED versions min(c/RV^2, 1) and min(c/RV^2, 1.5).
- 关键结果：Market factor 1926-2015: annualized alpha 4.86% (s.e. 1.56), beta 0.61, appraisal ratio 0.34; buy-and-hold Sharpe 0.42 -> 0.51 managed (~25% gain). Momentum alpha 12.51%; carry 2.78%; SMB is the one failure (-0.58). Mean-variance utility gain ~65% of lifetime utility. Weight distribution (Table V, Panel A): P50 0.93, P75 1.59, P90 2.64, P99 6.39 — substantial leverage needed in calm markets. WITH LEVERAGE CAP AT 1 (long-or-flat feasible): alpha drops to 2.12%/yr (s.e. 0.71, still significant), Sharpe 0.52, appraisal 0.30; cap at 1.5: alpha 3.10%. Utility gain with no-leverage constraint for a 60/40-target investor ~50%; ~45% with 50% margin for a 100%-stocks-target investor (Fig. 4). Transaction costs (Table IV): break-even cost 56bps baseline; 110bps for the cap-1 version (|delta w| falls from 0.73 to 0.16/month). Recession beta falls from 0.83 to 0.32 (Table III).
- 数据需求：Daily returns only (1 month of daily returns per estimate); monthly rebalance. Fully computable from our decades of Yahoo daily data.
- 局限：The alpha/utility framing assumes the ability to COMBINE managed and unmanaged portfolios with ex-post optimal weights (see Cederburg et al. 2020 — not implementable in real time). c is set with full-sample information (Liu-Tang-Zhou look-ahead critique); harmless for Sharpe but the reported return LEVEL is not a real-time number. Gains concentrate in a few high-vol episodes (1930s, 2008); subsample 1956-1985 alpha only 2.06 (t~0.73 for MVE combos). SMB fails. Direct Sharpe gain on the market is NOT statistically significant in direct comparison (see COWY Table 1: diff 0.09, p=0.30).

### Cederburg, S., O'Doherty, M.S., Wang, F. & Yan, X.S. (2020), "On the performance of volatility-managed portfolios", Journal of Financial Economics 138(1), 95-117
- 方法：Same construction f_sigma_t = (c*/sigma_hat_{t-1}^2) f_t applied to 103 strategies (MM's 9 factors + 94 anomaly portfolios). Three tests: (i) direct Sharpe comparisons (Jobson-Korkie); (ii) replication of spanning regressions; (iii) REAL-TIME combination strategies: estimate the ex-post-optimal scaled/unscaled weights (Eq. 10-11) on an expanding window (initial K=120 months), gamma=5, leverage cap sum|w| <= 5, then trade out of sample.
- 关键结果：Direct comparisons: managed wins in only 53/103 cases (p=0.84 vs 50/50 null); only 8 significantly positive, concentrated in momentum-type strategies (MOM +0.50 Sharpe, p=0.00; ROE +0.32; BAB +0.24; MKT +0.09 p=0.30 NOT significant; SMB -0.15). In-sample spanning alphas confirm MM: 77/103 positive [23 significant] — so the PAPER'S alphas replicate. But out of sample the real-time combination earns LOWER certainty-equivalent return than the plain unmanaged portfolio in 72/103 cases; for the market: real-time Sharpe 0.42 vs 0.46 for unmanaged-only, CER 1.56% vs 1.75% (Table 5). Cause: structural instability in spanning-regression parameters; required leverage 99th percentile >400% (up to 864% for MOM). Robust to rolling vs expanding windows, training length, risk aversion, leverage constraints.
- 数据需求：Monthly strategy returns plus daily returns for RV; long histories (their tests start 1926/1963).
- 局限：The failure is about the SPANNING-REGRESSION combination trade, not about de-risking per se: managed MOM/ROE/BAB still add value OOS. Their real-time design is mean-variance with gamma=5 and allows leverage to 5x — not our setting. Key transferable lesson: do not trust in-sample alpha/Sharpe gains from vol scaling on a broad-market long portfolio; the honest expectation for MKT-like assets is Sharpe roughly unchanged.

### Barroso, P. & Santa-Clara, P. (2015), "Momentum has its moments", Journal of Financial Economics 116(1), 111-120
- 方法：Risk-managed momentum: r_WML*_t = (sigma_target / sigma_hat_t) * r_WML_t (Eq. 6), i.e. scale by INVERSE VOL (not variance) to a constant vol target. sigma_target = 12% annualized. Variance forecast sigma_hat_t^2 = 21 * (sum of last 126 daily squared WML returns)/126 (Eq. 5) — 6 months of daily returns, refreshed monthly. Footnote 14: 1-month, 3-month, and EWMA (1/3/6-month half-life) estimators all give nearly identical results. No cap imposed; realized weights range 0.13 to 2.00, average 0.90.
- 关键结果：1927-2011 US WML: Sharpe 0.53 -> 0.97 (info ratio of managed vs raw = 0.78); mean return +2.04pp/yr with 10.58pp/yr LESS standard deviation. Excess kurtosis 18.24 -> 2.68; skewness -2.47 -> -0.42. Worst month -78.96% -> -28.40%; max drawdown -96.69% -> -45.20%. Vol of momentum is highly forecastable: AR(1) on monthly RV has OOS R^2 = 57.82%. Turnover barely rises (74% -> 75%/month) because sigma_hat has AR(1) coeff 0.97; break-even round-trip cost 1.27pp raw vs 1.75pp managed. Works in FR, DE, JP, UK subsamples.
- 数据需求：Daily strategy returns for 6-month RV window; monthly rebalance. Trivial with our daily history.
- 局限：This is the best-replicated result in the literature (COWY confirm: +0.50 Sharpe, significant), but it is specific to LONG-SHORT momentum, whose vol spikes predict its own crashes (negative risk-return relation within the strategy). It does NOT transfer to a long-only market portfolio, where the risk-return relation is flat. Requires up to 2x leverage on the long-short book; unavailable to us; capping changes the arithmetic. We hold no momentum factor — relevance is the vol-forecastability evidence and the estimator recipe.

### Harvey, C.R., Hoyle, E., Korgaonkar, R., Rattray, S., Sargaison, M. & van Hemert, O. (2018), "The Impact of Volatility Targeting", Journal of Portfolio Management 45(1), 14-33
- 方法：r_scaled_t = r_t * (sigma_target / sigma_{t-2}) * k, sigma known 24h ahead of trading; sigma_target = 10% annualized throughout; k~1 is a full-sample normalizer (source of the Liu-Tang-Zhou look-ahead critique — affects levels, not Sharpe). Vol estimator: exponentially weighted sd of daily returns, half-lives 10/20/40/60/90 days (270-day warm-up); also 5-minute realized vol for S&P 500 and 10Y Treasury futures since 1988. DAILY rebalancing; 60+ assets, some from 1926.
- 关键结果：Equities All U.S. 1927-2017 (Exhibit 5): Sharpe 0.40 unscaled -> 0.48-0.51 scaled (all half-lives); vol-of-vol 4.6% -> 1.7-2.2%; 1% left tail -11.4% -> -8.3% (10d half-life); turnover 4.66x/yr (10d) down to 0.56x (90d); mean notional exposure only 67-71%. S&P futures 1988-2017 (Exhibit 8): SR 0.50 -> 0.57-0.60 with daily-data vol, -> 0.59-0.63 with 5-MINUTE realized vol — intraday info adds only ~0.02-0.03 Sharpe over daily estimates. U.S. bonds 1963-2017: SR 0.25 -> 0.05-0.09 (vol targeting HURTS — high-vol quintile has the HIGHEST next-month bond returns, Exhibit 10); Treasury futures 1988-2017: 0.64 -> 0.63 (neutral). 60/40 portfolio 1988-2017 (Exhibit 16): SR 0.80 -> 0.87 (asset-level scaling) -> 0.91 (asset+portfolio level); 1% left tail -9.7% -> -7.3%. Mechanism (Exhibits 18-20): only "risk assets" show the leverage effect — corr(ret_t, delta var_t) ~ -0.2 to -0.35 for equities/credit, ~0/positive for bonds/FX/commodities; vol scaling therefore embeds ~1-month time-series momentum, and the cross-asset Sharpe improvement is explained by corr(R_21d, 1/vol) with R^2 = 45-60% (Exhibit 20). Vol-of-vol and left tail improve for ALL assets; Sharpe improves only where the leverage effect exists.
- 数据需求：Daily returns (decades preferred); optional intraday bars for the vol estimate. Directly matches our data: EWMA on daily Yahoo data for full history; hourly bars can proxy the intraday RV upgrade for the last 730 days.
- 局限：k is full-sample (look-ahead in levels); results are for futures with ~free leverage (mean exposure up to 186%) — a cap at 1 removes the lever-up-in-calm half of the trade, so expect smaller Sharpe effect but retained tail benefit. Bongaerts et al. show the ex-post scaling also understates realized drawdowns of the implementable version. Sharpe gains net of costs assume 1bp futures costs.

### Bongaerts, D., Kang, X. & van Dijk, M. (2020), "Conditional Volatility Targeting", Financial Analysts Journal 76(4), 54-71
- 方法：Implementable, no look-ahead: sigma_target = expanding-window long-term realized vol of the asset (>=10yr warm-up); sigma_hat_{t-1} = realized vol of daily returns in month t-1 (known 1 day before month-end rebalance). CONVENTIONAL: r_scaled = r_t * sigma_target/sigma_hat_{t-1} every month (Eq. 2). CONDITIONAL (Eq. 3): scale ONLY when last month's vol is in the TOP quintile (de-risk) or BOTTOM quintile (lever up to L_max) of its own expanding history; otherwise hold unscaled exposure. Leverage cap L_max = 2.0 (they state results robust to other caps); they ALSO test an UNLEVERED variant that only de-risks in high-vol states and never exceeds 1 — i.e., exactly our long/flat constraint. Monthly rebalance; all results NET of costs (3-5bp futures; 25bp factor-leg trading).
- 关键结果：Conventional targeting on 10 equity markets 1982-2019 (Table 1): avg Sharpe gain only +0.04; INCREASES max drawdown in UK/Canada/Australia/HK (+4.0 to +34.4pp); realized vol overshoots target (ratio 1.16 US, 1.31 UK); turnover 1.8-2.6x/yr. Conditional (Table 3): Sharpe gain avg +0.07 (US +0.16, significant), max drawdown REDUCED in 9/10 markets (avg -6.6pp), expected shortfall -1.3pp, realized/target vol 0.98, turnover 1.4x (~half of conventional), max leverage 2.0 vs 5.5. US momentum factor: Sharpe 0.14 -> +0.17 gain (doubles), maxDD -6.8pp, at half the turnover. Global momentum factors 1995-2019: Sharpe +0.23 avg, maxDD -20.1pp (from 54.1%), ES -9.4pp. UNLEVERED conditional momentum: raises US momentum excess return from 1.2% to 4.5%/yr (Figure 1) while lowering realized vol — de-risking in high-vol states alone carries most of the benefit. Mechanism (Table 2): corr(RV_{t-1}, r_t) across equity markets is -0.10 in high-vol states vs ~0.00 elsewhere; vol autocorrelation 0.52 high-vol vs 0.14/0.23 medium/low — predictability and the negative risk-return relation live in the HIGH-VOL STATE only.
- 数据需求：Daily returns with >=10 years of history to define vol quintiles and target without look-ahead. Our daily data suffice; quintile thresholds must be computed on expanding windows.
- 局限：Sharpe gains significant only in US and HK for markets; monthly state classification is coarse (a within-month crash like Feb 2020 is caught late); published on 1982-2019, includes 1987/2008 — only a handful of independent high-vol episodes drive the result, so validation on short windows is low-power by construction.

### Liu, F., Tang, X. & Zhou, G. (2019), "Volatility-Managed Portfolio: Does It Really Work?", Journal of Portfolio Management 46(1), 38-51
- 方法：Re-examines MM2017/Harvey2018 market-timing rules after removing the ex-post scaling constant c (which uses full-sample information): c must be estimated in real time from an expanding window. Also tests three alternative vol-timing rules on the US market.
- 关键结果：Real-time versions of the vol-managed MARKET portfolio have max drawdowns of 68-93% in almost all configurations and outperform buy-and-hold only around the 2008 crisis window; none of the alternative vol-timing strategies beats the market either.
- 数据需求：Same as MM: daily returns, monthly rebalance, plus an expanding-window estimate of c.
- 局限：The drawdown blow-ups come from the real-time c mis-scaling exposure UP (leverage) after calm periods; a hard cap at 1 mechanically removes that failure mode — this critique is mostly an argument FOR the capped/long-flat implementation, not against de-risking. Venue is practitioner (JPM), lower bar than JFE, but the look-ahead point is independently confirmed by Bongaerts et al. (2020).

### Barroso, P. & Detzel, A. (2021), "Do limits to arbitrage explain the benefits of volatility-managed portfolios?", Journal of Financial Economics 140(3), 744-767
- 方法：Recomputes MM-style spanning alphas for vol-managed equity factors NET of realistic transaction costs (effective spreads, price impact) and tests whether alphas concentrate in high-limits-to-arbitrage conditions; considers cost-mitigated designs (banding, lower rebalancing frequency).
- 关键结果：Net of transaction costs, vol-managed versions of most equity FACTORS (value, profitability, investment, BAB, etc.) lose their alpha; the market portfolio is the main case where net-of-cost vol management retains value because it trades one cheap, liquid instrument. Abnormal returns line up with limits-to-arbitrage proxies rather than exploitable free lunch.
- 数据需求：Factor daily returns plus cost estimates; not directly needed for us beyond the qualitative ruling.
- 局限：Cost estimates are for shorting-intensive factor legs; irrelevant to our zero-cost signal-validation stage, but it fixes the priority ordering: apply vol overlays to the INDEX/single-stock layer (cheap), never plan on factor-level overlays surviving retail costs.

### Bollerslev, T., Hood, B., Huss, J. & Pedersen, L.H. (2018), "Risk Everywhere: Modeling and Managing Volatility", Review of Financial Studies 31(7), 2729-2773
- 方法：HAR-family realized-volatility forecasting models estimated in a panel across 50+ futures using 5-minute high-frequency data over two decades; evaluates vol-targeting economic value via a utility framework comparing RV-model-based targeting vs simpler estimators.
- 关键结果：Realized-vol dynamics are strikingly similar within and across asset classes (near-identical HAR coefficients), enabling panel estimation; HF-based RV forecasts beat daily-return-based (e.g., rolling/EWMA) forecasts out of sample, and the improvement translates into positive but MODEST utility gains for vol-targeting investors — the big step is targeting at all, the small step is a better vol forecast. Consistent with Harvey et al. Exhibit 8/13: 5-min RV adds only ~0.02-0.03 Sharpe over daily EWMA on S&P futures and ~0.01-0.02 on Treasuries.
- 数据需求：Years of intraday bars per asset for full replication — we have only 60 days of 5-min and 730 days of hourly, so the HAR-panel approach is NOT replicable on our history; a reduced form (HAR on daily RV built from hourly bars, last 730 days) is feasible but unvalidatable across regimes.
- 局限：Futures universe, unconstrained leverage, institutional costs; the panel trick (borrowing strength across assets) is the transferable idea for our 35-ticker universe — estimate one shared vol model, not 35.

**对本项目的适用性**：CONCRETE RECOMMENDATION FOR OUR CONSTRAINTS (18 surviving US mega-cap tech + ETFs, long-only/long-flat, no leverage, daily-to-hourly decisions, zero costs at signal stage, target >=30% ann. return with <=20% maxDD on a validation window).

1) What the literature actually licenses under a leverage cap of 1. With w_t = min(c/RV_t^2, 1) the ONLY published long-history number is Moreira-Muir Table V: alpha falls from 4.86% to 2.12%/yr, Sharpe stays 0.52 (vs 0.42 buy-and-hold), and the strategy becomes pure de-risking (median uncapped weight is 0.93, so the cap binds in roughly half of all months — every 'lever up in calm markets' month is truncated). Expected consequences for us: (a) average exposure < 1, so expected RETURN is at or below buy-and-hold of the same sleeve — the vol overlay is a DRAWDOWN-CONTROL device, not a return source; the 30% return target must come from the underlying sleeve (concentrated tech beta), not from the overlay; (b) the reliable, replicated benefits at cap 1 are tail/vol-of-vol/drawdown reduction (Harvey: 1% left tail -11.4% -> ~-8.3%, vol-of-vol roughly halved; Bongaerts conditional: maxDD -6.6pp avg across markets, momentum maxDD -20pp) — this is exactly the tool for the <=20% maxDD constraint; (c) do NOT expect an out-of-sample Sharpe gain on a long index sleeve — Cederburg et al.: managed beats unmanaged in only 53/103 direct comparisons, market Sharpe diff +0.09 with p=0.30, and real-time combination versions lose in 72/103 cases.

2) Which rule to implement. Use the Bongaerts et al. CONDITIONAL, UNLEVERED variant as the regime layer — it is the only published design that maps 1:1 to our constraints (no look-ahead, no leverage, monthly-or-faster, net-of-cost): hold sleeve weight 1 unless trailing realized vol (previous ~21 trading days, computed per asset or on QQQ as the sleeve proxy) is in the TOP quintile of its own expanding history; in that state scale exposure by sigma_target/sigma_hat (sigma_target = expanding-window long-run vol), floor at 0 (our long/flat). Their unlevered momentum version still moved returns +3.3pp/yr with lower vol, and the conditional design cuts turnover ~50% vs unconditional scaling and avoids the vol-target overshoot (realized/target 0.98 vs 1.16-1.31) that creates the extra drawdowns documented for conventional targeting. Estimator choice: EWMA of daily squared returns with 20-60d half-life (Harvey) or 1-month RV (MM/Bongaerts); Barroso-Santa-Clara footnote 14 says 1m/3m/6m/EWMA all give nearly identical results, so do not burn degrees of freedom tuning the window — preregister one (suggest EWMA half-life 20d, updated daily).

3) Where hourly data honestly fits. The published marginal value of intraday vol information is ~0.02-0.03 Sharpe (Harvey Exhibit 8: S&P futures 0.59-0.63 intraday vs 0.57-0.60 daily) plus a slightly better left tail at matched turnover. With 730 days of hourly bars (~7 bars/day) we can build daily RV from hourly returns for the last 2 years and use it as a FASTER trigger for the high-vol state (detect a vol spike 1-3 days earlier than a 21-day window). What we CANNOT do: validate a vol-overlay on hourly history alone. Vol-managed strategies earn their keep in a handful of episodes per DECADE (1930s, 1987, 2000-02, 2008, 2020, 2022); 730 days contains at most ~1 high-vol episode, so any hourly-only backtest has near-zero discriminating power — the regime layer must be fitted and validated on the DECADES of daily data, with the hourly-RV trigger tested only as an A/B refinement inside the last 730 days and reported as anecdotal. The 60-day 5-minute and 30-day 1-minute samples are useful only for measuring microstructure noise in the hourly RV estimator, not for backtesting.

4) Asset-specific warnings from the mechanism. The Sharpe benefit exists only where the leverage effect (negative corr between returns and vol innovations, ~-0.2 to -0.35 for equities) plus vol persistence hold — true for our tech stocks and QQQ/SPY/IWM/GDX; NOT true for bonds (Harvey: vol targeting cut bond Sharpe 0.25 -> 0.05-0.09 because high-vol months had the HIGHEST bond returns) — so do not apply the same overlay to TLT/IEF/SHY/BIL; and NEVER apply inverse-vol scaling to VXX/SQQQ/PSQ/SH/UUP, whose return-vol correlation structure is inverted or absent. Per Bollerslev et al., estimate ONE shared vol dynamic across the 18 tech names (panel/pooled) rather than 18 separate models.

5) Honest feasibility statement on the 30%/20% goal. Nothing in this literature produces 30% annualized from vol timing itself: at cap 1 the overlay historically ADDS ~2%/yr alpha at best (MM Table V) and usually subtracts a little return while removing a lot of drawdown. The plan that is consistent with the evidence: sleeve = concentrated mega-cap tech (which did >30%/yr in-sample partly BECAUSE the universe is survivorship-selected — the validation number will be inflated and must be labeled as such), overlay = conditional unlevered vol targeting to pull maxDD from the 30-50% range toward <=20% (published reductions: -6.6pp market avg, -7 to -21pp for high-vol strategies, worst-month -79% -> -28% for the extreme momentum case). Preregister the overlay parameters (quintile threshold, EWMA half-life, monthly vs weekly rebalance) BEFORE running, per our own second-round KDJ lesson: the win-rate-style criteria can be gamed by endogenous exit timing; evaluate this overlay on return AND drawdown jointly, with a calendar-rebalanced unconditional-exposure control arm."

## regimes

### Hamilton (1989), "A New Approach to the Economic Analysis of Nonstationary Time Series and the Business Cycle", Econometrica 57(2), 357-384
- 方法：Two-state Markov-switching autoregression on quarterly US real GNP growth, estimated by MLE; causal inference uses the Hamilton FILTER P(S_t | data up to t); the Kim smoother P(S_t | full sample) is retrospective only. Causal deployment = recursive re-estimation on expanding window plus filtered probabilities.
- 关键结果：Filtered probabilities on 1952Q2-1984Q4 closely reproduce NBER recession dates without using them (verified against the Econometric Society abstract and Hamilton's own survey). This is the canonical origin of every regime model below, but the headline chart everyone reproduces is full-sample-parameter, revised-data inference — not real time.
- 数据需求：Quarterly macro aggregate (GDP/GNP). Not computable from our frozen dataset at all (no macro series); relevant only as methodology ancestor.
- 局限：Hamilton himself (2011, below) documents that real-time performance is far worse than the in-sample chart. Business-cycle frequency is uselessly slow for a daily/hourly trading gate. Parameter estimates are unstable across vintages; the 1989 parameters were fit on 2004-vintage revised data in most reproductions.

### Hamilton (2011), "Calling recessions in real time", International Journal of Forecasting 27(4), 1006-1026 (also NBER WP 16162)
- 方法：Real-time protocol for the GDP-based Markov-switching indicator: wait one quarter for data revision, use one-quarter-smoothed probability, declare recession start only when P first exceeds 0.65, declare end when P falls below 0.35; parameters and inference recomputed each release from vintage data (Croushore-Stark real-time database).
- 关键结果：Read directly from the paper PDF: full-sample inference looks excellent, but 'use of data as actually available at the time resulted in a considerable deterioration of reliability' (Chauvet-Hamilton 2006 vintage experiment). Actual out-of-sample record: the algorithm declared the 2007Q4 peak in January 2009 — 13 months after the recession began and one month AFTER NBER's own December 2008 announcement (P was only 46.9% in Oct 2008; needed the -3.9% 2008Q4 print to hit 88.4%). Trough 2009Q2 declared April 2010. Chauvet-Piger monthly 4-indicator model (rule: 3 consecutive filtered P>80%) announced on 2009-01-01 that a recession had begun in Feb 2008 — an 11-month recognition lag. NBER itself announced the 2001 recession's END 28 months late (July 2003).
- 数据需求：Vintage macro releases. Not available in our dataset.
- 局限：This is the definitive documented fragility record for Markov-switching used causally: recognition lags of roughly 2-13 months even with correct models, driven by (a) filtered-vs-smoothed information loss, (b) data revisions, (c) deliberate waiting rules needed to suppress false positives. Any regime layer we build inherits a scaled-down version of this lag.

### Bulla, Mergner, Bulla, Sesboue, Chesneau (2011), "Markov-switching asset allocation: Do profitable strategies exist?", Journal of Asset Management 12(5), 310-321 (MPRA 21154)
- 方法：The closest published template to what we would build. 2-state Gaussian HMM on DAILY index log returns (DAX, DJIA, NASDAQ 100, Nikkei 225, S&P 500, from 1976/83/85). Causal loop verified from the paper: rolling window n=2000 daily obs (~8 years), refit HMM, take Viterbi state path, predict next-day state via transition matrix row, then pass predictions through a causal median filter over the last k=6 predicted states (cuts state changes 50-65%, e.g. S&P 134→46 switches). Long index in low-variance state, cash (3% fixed) in high-variance state; 10 bp one-way costs.
- 关键结果：Out-of-sample Table 5 (~23 years, 5,834 forecasts for S&P): S&P 500 index 8.37% p.a. / 16.5% SD / Sharpe 0.390 vs strategy 8.56% / 10.3% / 0.577 with 46 switches. DJIA 8.93/16.8/0.417 → 9.82/11.2/0.646. NASDAQ 6.82/32.0/0.272 → 8.63/14.0/0.464. Average across markets: volatility cut 41%, annualized excess return +18.5 bp (S&P) to +201.6 bp (Nikkei) AFTER costs. In-sample fitted persistences p11≈0.99, p22≈0.95-0.99.
- 数据需求：Only daily adjusted closes — fully computable from our frozen SPY/QQQ/single-stock daily history. The 2000-day estimation window means the first usable signal comes ~8 years into a series; fine for SPY (1993+) and all decades-long stocks.
- 局限：Their own Figure 1 quantifies the filtered-vs-smoothed pitfall: state misclassification is ~3% mid-sample but rises to ~6.5-10% at the END of the sample — exactly where the trading decision sits. Return improvement is small (tens of bps); the value is almost entirely volatility/drawdown reduction. They explicitly cite Hess (2006) — with costs, regime-forecast CAPM strategies had NO advantage — and Dacco-Satchell. Gaussian components over-switch; Bulla (2011, Quantitative Finance 11:459-475) shows t-distributed components increase persistence and reduce false switches.

### Nystrup, Madsen, Lindstrom (2018), "Dynamic portfolio optimization across hidden market regimes", Quantitative Finance 18(1), 83-95
- 方法：2-state Gaussian HMM on daily log returns with ADAPTIVE (forgetting-factor, online) parameter estimation, effective memory ~2 years; stocks vs zero-interest cash, long-only; MPC optimizer on top. The rule-based comparator (RBAA) goes 100% to cash when the filtered probability of a regime change exceeds 0.9998. Allocation changes delayed one full day; 10 bp costs per transaction.
- 关键结果：MSCI World, out-of-sample 1998-2015, one-day delay (Table 2): buy-and-hold 5.6% p.a. / 18% SD / Sharpe 0.30 / MaxDD 57% vs MPC-HMM with trading penalty 6.9% / 11% / 0.63 / MaxDD 25% at only 1.17x annual turnover; threshold RBAA got 4.6% / 12% / 0.38 / MaxDD 34%. Applied to S&P 500 1986-2015 no-delay (Table 3): 11.0% vs 10.8% B&H return, Sharpe 0.79 vs 0.68, MaxDD 36% vs 55%. Worked on 5 of 6 indices (FTSE the exception).
- 数据需求：Daily closes only; the adaptive estimator needs ~2 years to warm up. Directly reproducible on our frozen daily data.
- 局限：MaxDD ~25% still exceeds our 20% cap on a plain index. Performance is sensitive to the trading-penalty hyperparameter; the fixed 0.9998 threshold variant (RBAA) timed 2008 well but badly lagged the 2009 rebound. Single team, one 18-year window; the one-day-delay test cost ~1pp of annual return versus same-close execution — decision-to-execution latency matters.

### Kritzman, Li (2010), "Skulls, Financial Turbulence, and Risk Management", Financial Analysts Journal 66(5), 30-41
- 方法：Turbulence index d_t = (y_t - mu) Sigma^-1 (y_t - mu)' (squared Mahalanobis distance) on a small panel of asset returns. Causal deployment verified from the paper's own trading test: mu and Sigma from a TRAILING 3-year window, 30-day moving average of daily turbulence, exposure scaled by which trailing quintile today's level falls in (100/80/60/40/20%), one-day implementation lag.
- 关键结果：Persistence (Table 1): after a top-decile turbulent day, average turbulence over the next 5/10/20 days still sits at the 4th-9th percentile from the top — turbulence persists up to a month, which is what makes a causal gate work. Risk assets earn much less per unit risk in turbulent periods (Fig 5: world equities positive annualized in quiet vs roughly -10 to -13% annualized in turbulent, 1993-2008). Causal scaling test on FX carry (Table 6): unfiltered return 2.81%, SD 5.55, IR 0.51, skew -0.48 → turbulence-scaled 3.02%, SD 3.29, IR 0.92, skew -0.15. Same rule using VIX as the signal: IR 0.50 — i.e. NO improvement over unconditional, versus 0.92 for turbulence.
- 数据需求：Daily returns for 5-10 diversified series with long history. Fully computable from our frozen ETFs (e.g. SPY, QQQ, IWM, TLT/IEF, GLD, UUP, XLP/XLU/XLV) from ~2007+ jointly, SPY/GLD-era subsets earlier.
- 局限：The headline turbulence chart (Fig 4) uses FULL-SAMPLE mu and Sigma — look-ahead; only the Table 6 recipe is causal. Effect size demonstrated on carry, not long-only equity. Turbulence is a relative measure: the turbulent/quiet threshold drifts as the world gets noisier. Equity-only panels (our case) partly collapse toward a fancy volatility measure.

### Kritzman, Page, Turkington (2012), "Regime Shifts: Implications for Dynamic Strategies", Financial Analysts Journal 68(3), 22-39 (corrected); numbers verified from Turkington's companion State Street deck, QWAFAFEW Boston, Aug 2011
- 方法：2-state Gaussian Markov-switching models fitted NOT to asset returns but to regime drivers: equity turbulence (1975+), currency turbulence (1977+), inflation (1947+), economic growth (1947+), monthly. Causal loop stated explicitly: each month re-calibrate by MLE on a GROWING window of data available to that point, tilt defensively when the filtered/forecast probability of the event regime is high, roll forward one month.
- 关键结果：In-sample event-regime persistences 68-95% (HMM) vs 46-62% for fixed thresholds; in a simulation, a threshold classifier misclassified 40/200 observations vs 3/200 for the HMM. Out-of-sample WITH 40bp costs: risk-premia portfolio Feb 1978-Dec 2009: static 5.99% excess / 8.37% vol / IR 0.72 / MaxDD -41.5% / skew -1.56 vs dynamic 6.28% / 6.83% / IR 0.92 / MaxDD -32.7% / skew -1.01, turnover ~1.5x/yr. Stock/bond dynamic asset allocation Feb 1973-Dec 2009: return 9.45%→9.29% (slightly LOWER), vol 9.88%→8.98%, worst year -34.9%→-29.5%, return-to-VaR 0.90→1.11, turnover 34%/yr.
- 数据需求：Monthly turbulence is computable from our ETF daily data; inflation/GNP legs are not in our dataset (and are too slow anyway).
- 局限：Gains are risk-shape gains, not return gains — the DAA return went down. Monthly frequency, ±5-10% tilts; nothing here supports a 30% CAGR ambition. Published erratum exists (2012) — use the corrected version. Backtest run by the model's inventors; no independent replication with the same drivers found.

### Faber (2007, update 2013), "A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation", Journal of Wealth Management 9(4); numbers verified from the paper PDF
- 方法：Price vs 10-month SMA on monthly closes: long the asset when the month-end close is above the 10-month SMA, else cash (T-bills). Zero estimated parameters (the causal-estimation question disappears); trades at next month-open in the stated protocol.
- 关键结果：S&P 500 1900-2005 (Table 2): buy-and-hold CAGR 9.75%, SD 19.91%, Sharpe 0.29, MaxDD -83.66% vs timing 10.66%, 15.38%, 0.43, MaxDD -49.98%; in the market 69.8% of months, 0.67 round-trips/yr, 63% of trades positive. Parameter stability (Table 4): 6/8/10/12/14-month SMAs all give CAGR 10.02-10.80% and MaxDD -44.7% to -56.1%. 1972-2005 S&P alone (Table 5): CAGR 11.18 vs 11.24 (B&H), but MaxDD -23.26% vs -44.73%. Five-asset-class version (Table 7): equal-weight B&H 11.57%/Sharpe 0.75/MaxDD -19.6% vs timed 11.92%/1.20/-9.5%.
- 数据需求：Monthly (or 200-day daily equivalent) closes only. Trivially computable on every ticker in our frozen set with decades of history.
- 局限：See Zakamulin (next) for the out-of-sample discount. Return distribution of timing is negatively skewed (misses best months, catches whipsaws); on single stocks the whipsaw cost is larger than on indices because idiosyncratic vol crosses the MA constantly. Roughly half the drawdown, roughly index-level CAGR is the honest expectation — not more.

### Zakamulin (2014), "The real-life performance of market timing with moving average and time-series momentum rules", Journal of Asset Management 15(4), 261-278; numbers verified from the SSRN manuscript
- 方法：Out-of-sample test of SMA(k) and time-series momentum MOM(k) with realistic costs: expanding-window rule selection (best k so far), applied to the next period, 1926-2012 S&P Composite, DJIA, and 2 bond indices; plus a stationary block-bootstrap (Politis-Romano, mean block 16 months) to estimate expected real-life performance.
- 关键结果：Best-in-backtest SMA(10) Sharpe looks ~50% above buy-and-hold; the out-of-sample, cost-inclusive version is only ~10% above (monthly Sharpe 0.12 vs 0.11, 1930-2012), and terminal wealth is 2-3x LOWER than buy-and-hold ($64k vs $166k per $100, Panel A). OOS MaxDD -61.7% vs B&H -79.2% — drawdown protection survives but is far weaker than the in-sample -33.7%. Bootstrap robust estimate for SMA on S&P: mean return -20%, SD -32%, Sharpe +7% relative to B&H; MOM: Sharpe +0%. ALL outperformance is confined to 4 short episodes: 1930-31, 1973-74, 2001-02, 2007-08; conclusions flip depending on whether the OOS split point sits before or after a major bear. 2009-2012: timing Sharpe 0.09-0.12 vs B&H 0.25.
- 数据需求：Same as Faber.
- 局限：This is the discount factor to apply to every trend-filter backtest, including ours: expect roughly +5-10% relative Sharpe, materially lower compounding than buy-and-hold in bull-heavy windows, and value that shows up only if the validation window contains a real bear market. A 2018-2026 tech-stock validation window (ours) contains 2022 — exactly one such episode; the estimate of gate value will have n=1 effective sample.

### Moreira, Muir (2017), "Volatility-Managed Portfolios", Journal of Finance 72(4), 1611-1644; and Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang, Yan (2020), "On the performance of volatility-managed portfolios", Journal of Financial Economics 138(1), 95-117
- 方法：Vol-managed weight = c / (previous month's realized variance from daily returns) — purely backward-looking, monthly rebalance. This is the academically cleanest 'volatility threshold/percentile regime' formalization (continuous version of a vol-percentile gate).
- 关键结果：Verified from the JF text: market-factor vol-managed portfolio earns alpha 4.9%/yr, appraisal ratio 0.33, ~25% increase in buy-and-hold Sharpe; mean-variance utility gain ~65%; works on Mkt, HML, Mom, RMW, ROE, IA, BAB, carry; robust (per authors) to costs and leverage limits. Cederburg et al. rebuttal on 103 equity strategies: the positive spanning-regression alphas are real but the implied trading strategy is NOT implementable in real time (c and the spanning weights need full-sample information); real-time out-of-sample versions generally earn LOWER certainty-equivalents and Sharpe than the unmanaged portfolios, due to structural instability of the spanning regression.
- 数据需求：Daily returns only. Directly computable on our data; with hourly bars the trailing RV estimate can be updated intraday for the last 730 days.
- 局限：The pair of papers is the single most important calibration for our vol-regime idea: the CONDITIONAL fact (high yesterday-vol → same mean, higher vol tomorrow) is solid; the PORTFOLIO improvement evaporates when every scaling constant is estimated causally. Fix: don't estimate c — use a fixed volatility target (Harvey et al., next) or a fixed percentile rule frozen at preregistration.

### Harvey, Hoyle, Korgaonkar, Rattray, Sargaison, van Hemert (2018), "The Impact of Volatility Targeting", Journal of Portfolio Management 45(1), 14-33
- 方法：Scale position to a constant (10%) annualized vol target using trailing realized volatility; 60 assets, daily data 1926-2017. No estimated parameters beyond the trailing RV window — fully causal by construction.
- 关键结果：Volatility targeting raises Sharpe ratios for RISK assets — equities and credit — but has negligible Sharpe impact for bonds, currencies, commodities; across all assets it reliably reduces the likelihood and severity of left-tail returns and drawdowns, because crashes occur at high vol when the targeted portfolio is small. (Won the JPM outstanding-article award; consistent with Moreira-Muir's conditional fact while avoiding their implementability trap.)
- 数据需求：Daily closes; trailing window 20-120 days. Trivial on our data.
- 局限：For a long-only no-leverage account the scaling is one-sided: we can only de-risk below 100%, never lever up in calm regimes, so the Sharpe gain is smaller than published (published results assume symmetric scaling). Effect size on equities is a Sharpe improvement of order tenths, not a return multiplier.

### Copeland, Copeland (1999), "Market Timing: Style and Size Rotation Using the VIX", Financial Analysts Journal 55(4), 73-81
- 方法：Signal = percentage deviation of VIX from its trailing (75-day) mean — a causal, threshold-style VIX regime rule; trades style/size spread portfolios on subsequent days.
- 关键结果：Statistically significant leading indicator: on days after VIX rises, large-cap beats small-cap and value beats growth; after VIX falls, the reverse. Positive excess returns in both directions at 1-20 day horizons. Note this is the canonical VIX-threshold citation, but it validates ROTATION (long-short spreads), not long/flat market timing; Kritzman-Li (2010, Table 6) found a VIX-quintile exposure rule added nothing (IR 0.50 vs 0.51 unfiltered) where turbulence added a lot (0.92).
- 数据需求：A VIX level series. NOT in our frozen dataset: we froze VXX (futures ETN, current series' Yahoo history only from 2018, level distorted by roll yield — unusable as a level-threshold input) and no ^VIX. A VIX-threshold regime is therefore not causally reproducible from the frozen data.
- 局限：Beyond the data gap: VIX thresholds are near-duplicates of trailing realized-vol thresholds for gating purposes (correlation of VIX with 21-day RV is very high), and implied-vol level embeds a variance risk premium that shifts across decades, making fixed thresholds non-stationary.

### Dacco, Satchell (1999), "Why do regime-switching models forecast so badly?", Journal of Forecasting 18(1), 1-16
- 方法：Analytical + empirical study of regime-switching forecasting under MSE loss (FX application).
- 关键结果：Only a SMALL misclassification rate in forecasting which regime prevails is needed to lose the entire advantage of knowing the true regime-switching model; forecasts from the true RS model can have higher MSE than a random walk. Empirically RS models fit well in-sample and are usually beaten out-of-sample by random walks.
- 数据需求：None (methodological).
- 局限：This, plus Bulla's end-of-sample misclassification doubling (~3%→6.5-10%) and Hamilton's 12-month real-time lags, is the documented mechanism behind the standard failure mode: smoothed-probability backtests look clean, filtered real-time probabilities whipsaw. Any prereg for our regime layer must specify FILTERED probabilities, a decision threshold with hysteresis (e.g. Hamilton's 0.65 enter / 0.35 exit or Bulla's median filter), and a one-bar execution delay.

**对本项目的适用性**：Concrete recommendation for our constraints (18 US tech mega/large caps + ETF set, Yahoo adjusted daily full history, hourly only 730d, 5-min 60d; long-only/long-flat, UK retail, daily-to-hourly decisions, zero costs at this stage; target >=30% ann. return with <=20% MaxDD on a validation window).

1. What the literature actually supports — effect sizes. Across every causally implemented study, a regime layer on an equity index buys roughly: volatility -30 to -45%, MaxDD cut by ~40-55% (Faber -83.7%→-50.0% over a century; -44.7%→-23.3% 1972-2005; Nystrup 57%→25%; Bulla vol -41%), Sharpe +0.1 to +0.25 absolute (Bulla 0.39→0.58 OOS; Nystrup 0.30→0.63), and CAGR change between -2pp and +2pp (Zakamulin's robust OOS estimate: mean return -20% relative, Sharpe only +7% relative). NO published causal regime method adds tens of points of CAGR. Therefore the 30% return target cannot come from the regime layer; it must come from the underlying universe (high-beta tech names), while the regime layer's only realistic job is to hold MaxDD <=20% — which is exactly what the literature says it can do (halving a QQQ-like 35% drawdown or a single-name 50-65% drawdown lands near, not safely inside, 20%; portfolio-of-names plus gate is more plausible than gate-on-single-name).

2. Feasibility ranking on our frozen data. (a) Trend filter (Faber 10-month SMA / 200-day MA): zero estimated parameters, decades of daily history on every ticker — implement first as the baseline gate, and apply Zakamulin's discount when reading our own backtests (all gate value will come from 2022 in a 2018-2026 validation window; effective n≈1 bear episode; do not tune k). (b) Realized-vol percentile/target gate: trailing 20-60d RV vs expanding-window percentile, or Harvey-style fixed vol target; fully causal; the Moreira-Muir alpha (4.9%/yr, appraisal 0.33) is the in-sample ceiling and Cederburg's rebuttal is the binding caveat — freeze all scaling constants/percentiles at preregistration, never fit them to the validation window; long-only truncation (can only de-risk) halves the published benefit. (c) Kritzman-Li turbulence: fully causal recipe published (trailing 3-yr mean/cov, 30-day MA, quintile scaling, 1-day lag); computable on our ETF panel (SPY QQQ IWM TLT IEF GLD UUP XLP XLU XLV) from ~2007 jointly; adds cross-asset correlation-breakdown information a pure vol gate misses; effect size documented on carry (IR 0.51→0.92), unproven for long-only equity — treat as candidate, prereg required. (d) 2-state Gaussian HMM on daily returns, Bulla protocol (rolling 2000-day refit, filtered/Viterbi state, k=6 median filter, next-day execution, enter/exit hysteresis): implementable and the best-documented daily-frequency Markov method (+18.5 to +201.6 bp p.a. after 10bp costs, vol -41%); expect the end-of-sample misclassification problem (3%→6.5-10%) to eat part of it. (e) Hamilton macro MS: not implementable (no macro data) and disqualified by its own real-time record (~12-month recognition lags). (f) VIX threshold: NOT reproducible — no ^VIX in the frozen set and VXX (2018+, roll-decayed) is not a level proxy; substitute the RV-percentile gate, which the evidence (Kritzman-Li Table 6: VIX filter IR 0.50 vs unfiltered 0.51) suggests loses nothing.

3. Straight talk on 730 days of hourly data. ~5,000 hourly bars is enough observations to FIT a 2-state variance-switching HMM, but 730 calendar days span at most one macro regime cycle, so hourly-fitted regime parameters cannot be validated across regimes — any hourly-only regime layer is unvalidatable by construction (Zakamulin: timing value lives in 4 episodes per 87 years). The defensible architecture is: estimate AND validate the regime layer on daily data over decades; use hourly bars only as (i) a faster-updating input to the same daily-frequency gate (e.g., intraday-updated realized vol so a vol-percentile gate can trigger at today's close instead of tomorrow's), and (ii) execution timing. Claim (i) as an implementation refinement, not a validated signal — its incremental value cannot be measured with 2 years of data and must not be preregistered as a performance claim. The 60-day 5-minute and 30-day 1-minute samples are useless for regime work; at most they calibrate the intraday RV estimator's noise.

4. Preregistration essentials this survey dictates: filtered (never smoothed) probabilities; expanding or rolling refit schedule fixed in advance; hysteresis thresholds fixed in advance (0.65/0.35-style, or median filter k fixed); one-bar execution delay; report the gate's marginal contribution against the ungated portfolio on the SAME window, and report bear-episode count in the validation window (it will be ~1, and the ruling should say so). Given the project's second-round lesson that win-rate criteria are gameable by endogenous stopping, judge the regime layer on return/MaxDD/Calmar, not hit rate."

## realized-vol

### Andersen, T.G. & Bollerslev, T. (1998), "Answering the Skeptics: Yes, Standard Volatility Models Do Provide Accurate Forecasts", International Economic Review 39(4), 885-905
- 方法：Defines realized variance (RV) from intraday returns as the ex-post volatility proxy for forecast evaluation. Shows Mincer-Zarnowitz R2 of a GARCH forecast is mechanically tiny when the proxy is the daily squared return (var(r^2)=2*sigma^4 measurement noise), and rises to its true level when the proxy is RV = sum of squared intraday returns. Under constant intraday vol, var(RV_m) = 2*sigma^4/m for m intraday returns, i.e., RV with m bars is ~m times more efficient than one squared daily return.
- 关键结果：Daily-horizon MZ R2 with squared-return proxy is close to zero (DM/$ GARCH(1,1) approx 0.047 in the original tables - number recalled, not re-verified today); with 5-minute RV as proxy the same forecasts reach R2 'as high as fifty percent at the daily horizon' (Bollerslev's own 2022 retelling, GARCH_Musings.pdf, public.econ.duke.edu, section 3, confirms 'close to zero' vs 'fifty percent').
- 数据需求：Intraday returns (5-30 min) to build the RV proxy; daily returns for the forecasting model.
- 局限：The errors-in-variables logic cuts both ways for us: any regime classifier scored against squared daily returns will look falsely weak; score against RV (or a range proxy) instead. Constant-intraday-vol efficiency arithmetic ignores diurnal U-shape and microstructure noise.

### Andersen, T.G., Bollerslev, T., Diebold, F.X. & Labys, P. (2001), "The Distribution of Realized Exchange Rate Volatility", JASA 96(453), 42-55; and (2003) "Modeling and Forecasting Realized Volatility", Econometrica 71(2), 579-625
- 方法：RV_t = sum_{i=1..m} r_{t,i}^2 over 30-min returns converges to quadratic variation (integrated variance + squared jumps) as m grows; treat log RV as an observed Gaussian time series and forecast it with a long-memory (fractionally integrated) trivariate VAR ('VAR-RV'), then compare against daily GARCH(1,1), RiskMetrics, and a VAR on log absolute returns via Mincer-Zarnowitz regressions.
- 关键结果：From the BIS-hosted full text (bis.org/cgfs/Diebold-et-al.pdf, Table 3A, in-sample 1-day-ahead MZ R2 on realized volatility, Dec-1987 to Dec-1996): VAR-RV 0.353 (DM/$), 0.374 (Yen/$), 0.505 (Yen/DM) vs GARCH(1,1) 0.261 / 0.295 / 0.389, RiskMetrics 0.262 / 0.263 / 0.368, VAR-ABS 0.152 / 0.153 / 0.299. Adding GARCH or RiskMetrics to VAR-RV raises R2 by <=0.005 (0.353->0.356 etc.): daily-return models carry almost no incremental information once RV is used. Log RV is approximately Gaussian with long memory d approx 0.4.
- 数据需求：Years of intraday (30-min) prices; 10 years used in the paper.
- 局限：FX, 24h market - no overnight-gap problem, unlike our equities. The 2003 model (fractional integration) is dominated in practice by HAR (Corsi 2009) which is OLS-estimable. Sampling at 30 min was chosen to dodge microstructure noise; for mega-cap US equities 5-min is standard (Liu-Patton-Sheppard 2015).

### Andersen, T.G., Bollerslev, T., Diebold, F.X. & Ebens, H. (2001), "The Distribution of Realized Stock Return Volatility", Journal of Financial Economics 61(1), 43-76
- 方法：Same RV construction applied to individual US equities: 5-min returns on the 30 DJIA stocks (sample Jan 1993 - May 1998, ~1,366 days - period recalled, medium confidence). Documents distributional facts for single-stock realized vol.
- 关键结果：Realized variance distributions are extremely right-skewed; log realized volatility is close to Gaussian; realized vol has slowly decaying autocorrelations consistent with long memory (d approx 0.35-0.4); daily returns standardized by realized standard deviation are approximately N(0,1). These are the exact stylized facts a regime layer should exploit (classify on log-vol, not vol).
- 数据需求：5-min intraday prices for each stock.
- 局限：Only large liquid Dow names - which is good for us: our 18 tickers are the same liquidity class. Direct numbers (kurtosis tables) not re-verified online today; the normality-of-standardized-returns finding is independently confirmed in Molnar (2012) using daily OHLC only.

### Corsi, F. (2009), "A Simple Approximate Long-Memory Model of Realized Volatility", Journal of Financial Econometrics 7(2), 174-196
- 方法：HAR-RV, exact spec (paper Eq. 8, Table 2 header, verified from full text at statmath.wu.ac.at): RV_{t+1d}^{(d)} = c + beta_d*RV_t^{(d)} + beta_w*RV_t^{(w)} + beta_m*RV_t^{(m)} + omega_{t+1d}, where RV_t^{(w)} = (1/5)*sum_{i=0..4} RV_{t-i}^{(d)} and RV_t^{(m)} = (1/22)*sum_{i=0..21} RV_{t-i}^{(d)}. Plain OLS with Newey-West (lag 5) SEs. RV built with the Zhang-Ait-Sahalia-Mykland (2005) two-scales estimator on tick data. Multi-horizon versions regress the h-day aggregated RV on the same three regressors.
- 关键结果：Table 2 (in-sample, 1-day): USD/CHF c=1.121, beta_d=0.352, beta_w=0.323, beta_m=0.235; S&P500 futures c=0.781, beta_d=0.372, beta_w=0.343, beta_m=0.224; T-Bond c=1.494, beta_d=0.039 (t=1.67, insignificant), beta_w=0.412, beta_m=0.361. Table 5 (out-of-sample, rolling 1000-day re-estimation, MZ R2): 1-day HAR 0.551 (USD/CHF), 0.696 (S&P500), 0.264 (T-Bond) vs AR(1) 0.497 / 0.640 / 0.152 and ARFIMA 0.546 / 0.651 / 0.256; at 2 weeks HAR 0.570 / 0.627 / 0.438 vs AR(3) 0.316 / 0.408 / 0.316. HAR matches long-memory ARFIMA at a fraction of the complexity and beats short-memory AR at every horizon.
- 数据需求：Only a daily RV (or any daily variance-proxy) series long enough for 22-lag OLS - a few hundred observations minimum, thousands preferred.
- 局限：Coefficients sum to approx 0.91 (near-unit persistence): forecasts are essentially a clever EWMA - gains over naive smoothing are real but modest at 1 day and grow with horizon. The regressor need not be tick-based RV: the HAR form is routinely and successfully applied to range-based proxies (large follow-on literature), which is precisely our use case.

### Barndorff-Nielsen, O.E. & Shephard, N. (2004), "Power and Bipower Variation with Stochastic Volatility and Jumps", J. Financial Econometrics 2(1), 1-37; and (2006) "Econometrics of Testing for Jumps in Financial Economics Using Bipower Variation", J. Financial Econometrics 4(1), 1-30
- 方法：Realized bipower variation BV_t = mu_1^{-2} * sum_{i=2..m} |r_{t,i}|*|r_{t,i-1}|, with mu_1 = sqrt(2/pi) approx 0.79788 (so mu_1^{-2} = pi/2 approx 1.5708). BV converges to integrated variance only, while RV converges to integrated variance + sum of squared jumps; hence jump component J_t = max(RV_t - BV_t, 0). 2006 paper gives the test: ratio statistic z = delta^{-1/2} * (1 - BV_t/RV_t) / sqrt(theta * max(1, TQ_t/BV_t^2)) ~ N(0,1) under no jumps, theta = pi^2/4 + pi - 5 approx 0.6090, TQ_t = realized tripower quarticity (mu_{4/3}^{-3} scaled sum of |r_i|^{4/3}|r_{i-1}|^{4/3}|r_{i-2}|^{4/3}).
- 关键结果：Provides the standard nonparametric jump/continuous decomposition. Follow-on HAR-RV-CJ work (Andersen-Bollerslev-Diebold 2007, REStat 89(4), 701-720) shows the continuous part is the persistent, forecastable part while jumps are nearly iid - separating them improves HAR forecasts modestly.
- 数据需求：Needs m large enough per day for the asymptotics: validated at 5-min sampling (m approx 78-288). Not meaningful at m=7 (our hourly bars).
- 局限：Microstructure noise biases BV upward relative to RV at very fine sampling (staggered/skip versions fix this). For us: a proper BNS test is only feasible on the 60-day 5-min sample - long enough to sanity-check jump frequency, far too short to build a jump-regime history.

### Huang, X. & Tauchen, G. (2005), "The Relative Contribution of Jumps to Total Price Variance", J. Financial Econometrics 3(4), 456-499
- 方法：Monte Carlo evaluation of the BNS z-statistics (linear, ratio, log versions with tripower quarticity and the max adjustment) plus empirical application; relative jump measure RJ_t = (RV_t - BV_t)/RV_t.
- 关键结果：Jumps account for approx 4.4-4.6% of total price variance of S&P 500 index futures 1982-2002 and approx 7.3% for the S&P cash index 1997-2002 (the widely quoted '7%'). Simulations: the ratio-max statistic at 5-min sampling has close-to-nominal size and high power.
- 数据需求：5-min (or finer) intraday returns over long samples.
- 局限：Since jumps are only ~5-7% of total variance and largely unforecastable, skipping a formal jump split costs little for vol-REGIME classification. Cheap daily-data stand-ins: overnight gap squared ln(O_t/C_{t-1})^2, and close-to-close-squared minus Garman-Klass as a crude jump-day flag (both are heuristics, not BNS tests - unverified as formal detectors).

### Parkinson, M. (1980), "The Extreme Value Method for Estimating the Variance of the Rate of Return", Journal of Business 53(1), 61-65
- 方法：Notation (used below): per day, u = ln(H/O), d = ln(L/O), c = ln(C/O), j = ln(O_t/C_{t-1}). Parkinson: sigma2_P = (ln(H/L))^2 / (4*ln2), from E[(range of BM)^2] = 4*ln2*sigma^2 (Feller 1951 range distribution). Multi-day estimate = average of daily values.
- 关键结果：Theoretical efficiency 4.9x vs the close-to-close squared-return estimator (verified in Molnar 2012 full text, mmquant.net PDF: 'Parkinson volatility estimator has efficiency 4.9').
- 数据需求：Daily H, L only.
- 局限：Assumes zero drift, continuous observation, no overnight gap. Discrete trading makes observed H below true high / observed L above true low -> downward bias; bid-ask bounce biases the other way; both small for liquid mega-caps (Molnar 2012). Ignores overnight variance entirely - must add j^2 for whole-day vol of equities.

### Garman, M.B. & Klass, M.J. (1980), "On the Estimation of Security Price Volatilities from Historical Data", Journal of Business 53(1), 67-78
- 方法：Practical estimator: sigma2_GK = 0.5*(u-d)^2 - (2*ln2 - 1)*c^2. Their exact minimum-variance analytic estimator is sigma2_GK* = 0.511*(u-d)^2 - 0.019*(c*(u+d) - 2*u*d) - 0.383*c^2 (cross-term negligible; formulas verified from Molnar 2012 Eqs 14-15). Whole-day (jump-adjusted) version: add + j^2 (Molnar Eq. 36).
- 关键结果：Efficiency 7.4x vs close-to-close for the practical estimator (Molnar 2012, line-verified); the exact analytic version adds only marginally (GK report up to approx 8.4 - recalled, verify against the 1980 paper before quoting onward). Molnar (2012) rates GK the best all-round daily OHLC estimator; Bali-Weinbaum (2005), Li-Hong (2011), Todorova-Husmann (2012), Jiang et al. (2014) reach the same verdict empirically.
- 数据需求：Daily O, H, L, C (+ prior close for the jump term).
- 局限：Zero-drift assumption (fine at daily horizon: |mean| << sd). CRITICAL implementation trap flagged by Molnar: substituting close-to-close ln(C_t/C_{t-1}) for c in the GK formula (done in Bollen-Inder 2002, Fiess-MacDonald 2002) makes the estimator MORE downward-biased and can go negative. Correct whole-day form is GK-on-intraday-terms + j^2.

### Rogers, L.C.G. & Satchell, S.E. (1991), "Estimating Variance from High, Low and Closing Prices", Annals of Applied Probability 1(4), 504-512
- 方法：sigma2_RS = u*(u-c) + d*(d-c). Unbiased for ANY drift (the only single-day OHLC estimator with this property).
- 关键结果：Efficiency 6.0x vs close-to-close at zero drift, and stays above 2x for arbitrary drift (Molnar 2012, line-verified). Building block of Yang-Zhang.
- 数据需求：Daily O, H, L, C.
- 局限：Same discreteness bias as all range estimators (Rogers-Satchell-Yoon 1994 give a correction). Ignores overnight variance; drift-robustness is a second-order benefit at daily frequency.

### Yang, D. & Zhang, Q. (2000), "Drift-Independent Volatility Estimation Based on High, Low, Open, and Close Prices", Journal of Business 73(3), 477-491
- 方法：Over an n-day window: sigma2_YZ = sigma2_o + k*sigma2_c + (1-k)*sigma2_RS, where sigma2_o = (1/(n-1))*sum(o_t - o_bar)^2 with o_t = ln(O_t/C_{t-1}) (the overnight term), sigma2_c = (1/(n-1))*sum(c_t - c_bar)^2 with c_t = ln(C_t/O_t), sigma2_RS = (1/n)*sum RS_t, and k = 0.34 / (1.34 + (n+1)/(n-1)). Minimum-variance unbiased in its class, independent of drift AND of opening jumps.
- 关键结果：Efficiency up to a maximum of 14x vs close-to-close (paper's headline number, echoed across secondary sources incl. portfoliooptimizer.io write-up). Efficiency is highest when overnight variance share is small and degrades toward ~1-2 as overnight variance dominates, because the sigma2_o term is itself just a squared-return estimator (structural observation, not a paper quote). Shu-Zhang (2006): YZ is the ONLY range estimator unbiased under large opening jumps.
- 数据需求：Daily O, H, L, C with prior closes; a window n >= 2 (n ~ 10-63 typical).
- 局限：Estimates AVERAGE variance over the n-day window - it cannot produce a single-day estimate (Molnar 2012 excludes it from single-day comparisons for exactly this reason). For US equities the overnight share is substantial (~20-40% of daily variance for large caps - rough literature range, unverified precise figure), so realized YZ efficiency is well below 14. Dividend-adjusted Yahoo prices distort o_t slightly on ex-div dates.

### Alizadeh, S., Brandt, M.W. & Diebold, F.X. (2002), "Range-Based Estimation of Stochastic Volatility Models", Journal of Finance 57(3), 1047-1091
- 方法：Shows the log range is a nearly Gaussian, highly efficient vol proxy: ln(range) approx N(0.43 + ln sigma, 0.29^2) vs ln|return| approx N(-0.64 + ln sigma, 1.11^2); uses log range as the measurement equation in QMLE Kalman-filter estimation of two-factor SV models on FX.
- 关键结果：Proxy noise s.d. 0.29 vs 1.11 - the range is ~4x less noisy in log space (equivalently ~14x in variance of the measurement error), and near-Gaussianity makes Gaussian filtering/regime machinery legitimate on log-range. Also shows the range is robust to bid-ask bounce and discreteness relative to high-frequency RV.
- 数据需求：Daily H, L (O, C for extensions).
- 局限：FX futures, not equities; single-factor log-vol AR(1) dynamics are too simple (they need two factors). For us the actionable content is: build the regime classifier on LOG of a range-based estimator - the near-Gaussian 0.29-noise measurement is what makes decades of OHLC an adequate substitute for HF data.

### Molnar, P. (2012), "Properties of Range-Based Volatility Estimators", International Review of Financial Analysis 23, 20-29
- 方法：Full-text verified (mmquant.net PDF). Systematic simulation + empirical study of Parkinson, GK, GK-precise, Meilijson, RS under their own ideal assumptions; correct handling of opening jumps (add j^2); empirical test on the 30 DJIA stocks (CRSP daily OHLC, 1992-2008, 4,171 obs/stock) of whether returns standardized by range-based sd are Gaussian, replicating the ABDE (2001) HF-data result.
- 关键结果：Efficiencies: Parkinson 4.9, GK 7.4, Meilijson 7.7, RS 6.0 (zero drift). Verdict: 'for most purposes, the best volatility estimator is the Garman-Klass volatility estimator'; Meilijson's extra 0.3 efficiency not worth the complexity. Empirically, daily returns standardized by jump-adjusted GK sd are approximately Gaussian for DJIA stocks - 'basically the same results from daily data as ABDE (2001) obtained from high-frequency (transaction) data', while GARCH-based sd leaves fat tails. This is the single most direct published evidence that daily OHLC proxies track HF vol for liquid US large caps - our exact universe class.
- 数据需求：Daily OHLC only.
- 局限：Reports no correlation table of GK vs 5-min RV per stock (the normality replication is the evidence). Constant-intraday-vol assumption maintained. Published in IRFA (not a top-3 journal) but heavily cited and internally verifiable.

### Shu, J. & Zhang, J.E. (2006), "Testing Range Estimators of Historical Volatility", Journal of Futures Markets 26(3), 297-313
- 方法：Monte Carlo under GBM with drift, opening jumps, and microstructure noise; empirical comparison of Parkinson, GK, RS, YZ against daily integrated variance on S&P 500 index data.
- 关键结果：All four perform well under pure GBM; under large drift Parkinson and GK OVERestimate; under opening jumps all except YZ are downward-biased; range estimators 'quite close to the daily integrated variance' on S&P 500 data and fairly robust to simulated microstructure noise.
- 数据需求：Daily OHLC; intraday data only for the benchmark.
- 局限：Index-level, not single stocks; exact correlation/MSE tables are behind the Wiley paywall (not re-verified today). Consistent with Molnar's ranking and with using YZ (or GK + j^2) when overnight gaps matter - which they do for US equities.

### Todorova, N. & Husmann, S. (2012), "A Comparative Study of Range-Based Stock Return Volatility Estimators for the German Market", Journal of Futures Markets 32(6), 560-586
- 方法：25 German (DAX) individual stocks; compares Parkinson, GK, RS, YZ and the realized range against a 5-min realized-variance benchmark, on bias and efficiency, including scaling corrections for discrete trading.
- 关键结果：GK is the optimal daily-OHLC estimator for individual equities (independent confirmation of Molnar/Bali-Weinbaum); the realized range (sum of intraday sub-period ranges, Christensen-Podolskij 2007 / Martens-van-Dijk 2007) beats daily-range measures but still suffers discrete-trading bias; daily range measures beat the naive intraday close-open measure.
- 数据需求：Daily OHLC; intraday only for benchmarking.
- 局限：Per-stock correlation numbers are paywalled - I could not extract them today; treat 'GK optimal, realized range better if intraday available' as the verified takeaway. This is the closest published single-equity analogue to our validation task; the actual correlations for OUR tickers should be computed in-house on the 730-day hourly overlap (better evidence than any German-market table).

### Liu, L.Y., Patton, A.J. & Sheppard, K. (2015), "Does Anything Beat 5-Minute RV? A Comparison of Realized Measures Across Multiple Asset Classes", Journal of Econometrics 187(1), 293-311
- 方法：Data-based ranking (Patton 2011 QLIKE/MSE framework with unbiased proxies) of ~400 realized measures (RV at many frequencies, subsampled RV, realized kernels, bipower, truncated, range-based realized measures...) on 31 assets over 11 years, including individual US equities.
- 关键结果：5-min RV is rarely significantly beaten by anything; 5-min subsampled RV and realized kernels are the only near-competitors; very fine sampling (1-min and below) HURTS for anything but the most liquid instruments, and coarse sampling (15-min+) loses accuracy steadily.
- 数据需求：Intraday prices.
- 局限：Directly calibrates our frozen data: the 60-day 5-min sample is the gold-standard benchmark window (use subsampled 5-min RV); our 730-day hourly RV (m approx 7 bars/day) is a WEAK measure - variance arithmetic gives it efficiency approx m = 7 vs a squared daily return, i.e., BARELY better than daily GK's 7.4 and worse than GK + j^2. Do not treat hourly RV as ground truth; treat it as a second proxy.

### Hansen, P.R. & Lunde, A. (2005), "A Realized Variance for the Whole Day Based on Intermittent High-Frequency Data", Journal of Financial Econometrics 3(4), 525-554
- 方法：Whole-day variance for equities as an optimally weighted combination sigma2_day = omega_1*r_overnight^2 + omega_2*RV_intraday, weights chosen to minimize MSE against the whole-day quadratic variation; applied to DJIA stocks.
- 关键结果：Simply adding r_overnight^2 to intraday RV is inadmissible (dominated); optimal weights differ from (1,1) because the overnight squared return is far noisier per unit of variance it carries. (Exact estimated weights not re-verified today - in the paper's tables.)
- 数据需求：Intraday RV plus overnight returns.
- 局限：The same logic is what Yang-Zhang's k does for daily data. For our hourly-RV validation series, combine overnight gap^2 with intraday hourly RV rather than concatenating; otherwise regime features will be dominated by overnight noise.

**对本项目的适用性**：RECOMMENDATION FOR OUR FROZEN DATA (18 US mega/large-cap tech + 17 ETFs, decades of Yahoo adjusted daily OHLCV, 730d hourly, 60d 5-min, 30d 1-min; long/flat, daily-to-hourly decisions).

(1) PRIMARY VOL PROXY FROM DECADES OF DAILY OHLC - this is fully supported by the literature. Compute per day, with u=ln(H/O), d=ln(L/O), c=ln(C/O), j=ln(O_t/C_{t-1}): jump-adjusted Garman-Klass GKJ_t = 0.5*(u-d)^2 - (2ln2-1)*c^2 + j^2 (Molnar 2012 Eq.36 - the j^2 term is mandatory for US equities; omitting it or using close-to-close inside GK are the two documented implementation traps). Secondary/robustness: Yang-Zhang on rolling n=21 days with k=0.34/(1.34+(n+1)/(n-1)) as the level estimator (only YZ is unbiased under opening jumps per Shu-Zhang 2006, but it cannot give single-day values). Work in LOG space: log range-based vol has approx Gaussian measurement noise of s.d. ~0.29 vs ~1.11 for log|return| (Alizadeh-Brandt-Diebold 2002), and log RV is near-Gaussian (ABDL 2001, ABDE 2001) - so regime classification (thresholds, HMM, quantiles) on log(GKJ smoothed) is statistically clean. Efficiency bookkeeping: GKJ approx 7.4x close-to-close, so one day of OHLC carries roughly a week of squared-return information; decades of OHLC therefore substitute for HF history remarkably well for THIS universe - Molnar (2012) demonstrated on the 30 DJIA stocks (1992-2008) that daily GK+j^2 reproduces the ABDE (2001) high-frequency normality result, which is the strongest published validation that daily OHLC tracks HF vol for liquid US large caps.

(2) SMOOTHING/FORECAST LAYER: fit HAR (Corsi 2009) on the daily GKJ (or log GKJ) series per ticker and on QQQ/SPY: X_{t+1} = c + b_d*X_t + b_w*mean(X_{t-4..t}) + b_m*mean(X_{t-21..t}). Expect coefficients near 0.35/0.30/0.25 summing to ~0.9 and 1-day out-of-sample Mincer-Zarnowitz R2 in the 0.5-0.7 range for index products (Corsi Table 5: 0.551 FX, 0.696 S&P futures), lower for single names. Plain OLS, re-estimated on a rolling window, matches ARFIMA - do not bother with fancier long-memory machinery. The three HAR components (1/5/22-day) are themselves natural regime features.

(3) WHAT THE 730 DAYS OF HOURLY BARS ARE FOR - be realistic: (a) hourly RV (m~7 bars/day) has theoretical efficiency ~7 vs a squared daily return, i.e., NO better than daily GKJ (7.4); it adds almost no per-day information beyond OHLC, so do NOT treat it as ground truth or as a richer regime input; (b) its real uses are: validating the daily proxy (compute corr(GKJ_t, overnight^2 + hourly RV_t) and rank correlations per ticker on the overlap - published per-stock correlation tables are scarce/paywalled (Todorova-Husmann 2012 is the closest), so this in-house validation IS the missing bridge evidence), splitting overnight vs intraday variance share per ticker (needed to know how far below 14x your YZ efficiency really is), and intraday updating of today's vol estimate at the hourly decision frequency (running Parkinson on today's partial-day H/L); (c) 730 days spans at most one-plus vol cycle (2024H2-2026Q3) - NOT enough to train or validate a regime model on hourly RV alone; train regimes on decades of GKJ, use hourly only for nowcasting.

(4) 5-MIN (60d) AND 1-MIN (30d): use subsampled 5-min RV as the gold-standard benchmark (Liu-Patton-Sheppard 2015: nothing reliably beats 5-min RV) to (a) estimate the per-ticker multiplicative bias of GKJ (range estimators are biased down by discreteness; expect a scale factor slightly above 1) and (b) run the BNS ratio-max jump test (BV = (pi/2)*sum|r_i||r_{i-1}|, z with theta = pi^2/4+pi-5 approx 0.609) to check jump frequency. A proper BNS jump history is INFEASIBLE with our data (needs years of 5-min bars; hourly m=7 is far below the validated regime of the asymptotics) - accept this. Jumps are only ~5-7% of total variance (Huang-Tauchen 2005), so for REGIME classification the loss is small; if a jump flag is wanted, use daily heuristics (large j^2; close-to-close^2 >> GK) and label them as heuristics.

(5) DATA HYGIENE SPECIFIC TO YAHOO OHLC: ratio-based estimators are invariant to split adjustment only if all four fields are scaled by the same factor per day - verify this on known split dates (NVDA 2021/2024, TSLA 2020/2022, AMZN/GOOGL 2022); dividend adjustment leaks into the overnight term j on ex-div dates (material for BIL/SHY/TLT/XLU monthly payers - compute j from unadjusted closes there if possible); VXX has repeated reverse splits and (with PSQ/SQQQ/SH/UUP/GDX) contaminated H/L prints are common on Yahoo - winsorize days where range/|close-to-close| is implausible; PLTR (2020-09) and DELL (2016-08 relisting) do not have 'decades' - the regime layer for those must borrow cross-sectionally (e.g., classify on QQQ/sector vol regime).

(6) HONESTY ABOUT THE GOAL: this literature establishes that daily OHLC gives you ~85-95% of the vol-regime information content of HF data for this universe (efficiency 7.4-14 vs the ~78+ of full 5-min RV, but with 4x-shorter-noise log proxies and decades of history), and that vol is highly forecastable (R2 0.5-0.7). It says NOTHING about whether a vol-regime layer plus long-only signals delivers >=30% annualized with <=20% MDD - that is a portfolio-construction question (vol-managed/vol-targeting literature, e.g. Moreira-Muir 2017, is the relevant next survey). The bridge itself - decades of OHLC standing in for HF vol - is sound and the recommended estimator stack is: GKJ daily + HAR smoothing + YZ level check, validated against overnight^2+hourly RV (730d) and bias-calibrated against subsampled 5-min RV (60d).

## trend-tsmom

### Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012), "Time Series Momentum", Journal of Financial Economics 104(2), 228-250. Verified from published PDF (w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/TimeSeriesMomentum.pdf)
- 方法：TSMOM: for each of 58 liquid futures (24 commodities, 9 equity indexes, 13 bonds, 12 FX), go long if past 12-month excess return > 0, short otherwise; hold 1 month; each position sized 40%/sigma_t-1 where sigma is an EWMA vol estimate (60-day center of mass), lagged to avoid look-ahead. Pooled panel regressions of vol-scaled monthly return on sign(past k-month return), t-stats clustered by month.
- 关键结果：Sample Jan 1965-Dec 2009 (strategy evaluation 1985-2009). Diversified TSMOM: annualized Sharpe > 1 gross (~2.5x the equity market's), 12%/yr realized vol; monthly alpha vs MSCI World+SMB+HML+UMD = 1.58%/month (t=7.99); vs Asness-Moskowitz-Pedersen everywhere factors still 1.09%/month (t=5.40). Every one of the 58 contracts has positive TSMOM returns; 52/58 significant at 5%. Alpha t-stats (Table 2), 12-month lookback/1-month hold: all assets 6.61; equity index futures only 3.77 (equity is among the weaker classes; 9-month lookback is stronger at t=4.21). Out-of-sample 1966-1985: Sharpe 1.1. "Smile": TSMOM return loads positively on market return squared (coef 1.99, t=3.88) - best in extreme up AND down quarters. Return continuation holds for lags 1-12 months, partial reversal beyond ~1 year.
- 数据需求：Monthly (or daily) total-return series and a rolling vol estimate. Fully computable from our decades of adjusted daily OHLCV for all 18 stocks + ETFs.
- 局限：Futures, long-AND-short, cross-asset diversified - none of which we can replicate long-only in single stocks; the Sharpe>1 headline comes mostly from diversification across ~58 lowly-correlated markets, not from per-asset predictability (per-instrument gross Sharpes in Fig. 2 are ~0.1-0.8). Gross of costs. Directly attacked by Huang et al. (2020) below.

### Hurst, Ooi & Pedersen, "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing", AQR whitepaper Fall 2012 (verified from PDF at chesler.us); peer-reviewed version: Journal of Portfolio Management 44(1), 2017, extended to 1880-2016, 67 markets, avg per-market Sharpe ~0.4
- 方法：Equal-weighted combination of 1-, 3-, and 12-month TSMOM over 59 markets (11 equity indices, 15 bonds, 24 commodities, 9 FX), always long-or-short each market, positions vol-targeted, combined portfolio scaled to 10% ex-ante vol; simulated transaction costs (equities one-way 0.36% pre-1993, 0.12% 1993-2002, 0.06% 2003-2012) and hypothetical 2/20 fees subtracted.
- 关键结果：Jan 1903-Jun 2012: 20.0%/yr gross of fees, 14.3%/yr net of 2/20, 9.9% vol, net Sharpe 1.00, correlation to S&P 500 -0.05. Net-of-fee Sharpe positive every decade (range 0.53-1.89; worst 1983-1992, best 1973-1982). Positive returns in 9 of the 10 largest 60/40 drawdowns 1903-2012 (Great Depression, 1973-74, dot-com, GFC). Strategy's own worst drawdown -26.3% (Mar 1947-Dec 1948); 2000s-crisis drawdowns -13.5% max. Adding 20% TSMOM to 60/40: return 8.0%->9.5%, vol 11.1%->9.0%, Sharpe 0.34->0.57, MaxDD -62%->-52%.
- 数据需求：Same as MOP: monthly returns + vol targeting. Computable from daily data; the crisis-episode conditional analysis is replicable on our SPY/QQQ/TLT set.
- 局限：AQR marketing-adjacent (hypothetical backtest, disclosed as such); includes shorts, which drive much of the bear-market convexity - a LONG-FLAT version keeps only part of the crisis benefit (it can go to cash, not profit from declines). Post-2009 decade was the weakest in-sample era cited (11.4% gross 2003-2012), and the paper itself flags rising cross-market correlations.

### Faber (2007, updated Feb 2013), "A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation", The Journal of Wealth Management (Spring 2007); numbers below verified from the 2013 update PDF (SSRN abstract 962461, mebfaber.com copy)
- 方法：Monthly close vs 10-month SMA on total-return series: long the asset when price > 10m SMA, else 90-day T-bills; evaluated once a month at month-end, ignoring intra-month moves; taxes/commissions/slippage excluded; also a 5-asset equal-weight version (GTAA: S&P 500, EAFE, US 10Y, GSCI, NAREIT).
- 关键结果：S&P 500 1901-2012: B&H CAGR 9.32%, vol 17.87%, Sharpe 0.32, MaxDD -83.46% (monthly basis), 61.58% positive months. TIMING: CAGR 10.18%, vol 11.97%, Sharpe 0.55, MaxDD -50.29%, 75.80% positive months, invested ~70% of the time, <1 round-trip/yr. Episodes: 1929-30s DD cut from -83.66% to -42.24%; 2000-02 exit Oct 2000, timing DD -16.52% vs -44.73%; 2008 exited Dec 31 2007, sidestepped the entire -50.95% bear (2008 year: timing +1.33% vs -36.77%). Whipsaw cost documented: 1973 timing -15.36% vs index -14.69% (timing WORSE in a down year); underperforms B&H in roughly half of all years. Parameter stability (GTAA 1973-2012): 3/6/9/12-month SMA returns 9.54-10.73%, Sharpe 0.60-0.77 vs B&H 0.44. GTAA 1973-2012: 10.48%/yr, vol 6.99%, Sharpe 0.73, MaxDD -9.54% vs B&H 9.92%, 10.28%, -46.00%. Out-of-sample 2006-2012: GTAA 6.01%/yr vs B&H 3.94%, MaxDD -9.42% vs -46.00%.
- 数据需求：Monthly total-return closes only. Trivially computable from our daily Yahoo adjusted data (adjusted close approximates total return).
- 局限：Not peer-reviewed beyond JWM (practitioner journal); author sells funds on the strategy. Return improvement over B&H is only ~+0.9pp/yr over 111 years - the product is drawdown reduction, not return enhancement. Zakamulin (2014) shows the 10-month choice carries data-mining bias and the out-of-sample edge is far smaller. Sub-50% DD, not sub-20%: even this rule left -42% in the 1930s and -50.29% max at monthly resolution.

### Levine & Pedersen (2016), "Which Trend Is Your Friend?", Financial Analysts Journal 72(3), 51-66
- 方法：Shows analytically and empirically that time-series momentum (past-return sign) and moving-average crossover rules are equivalent representations in their most general forms - both are weighted averages of past price changes with different weighting kernels; also nests HP filter, Kalman filter, and all linear filters.
- 关键结果：TSMOM and MA-crossover implementations of the 'same' trend horizon are near-perfectly correlated and deliver statistically indistinguishable performance; the choice among linear trend filters is second-order relative to the choice of horizon and risk sizing.
- 数据需求：None beyond daily closes.
- 局限：Implication for us: do NOT burn search-budget (multiple-testing risk) comparing SMA vs EMA vs MACD vs crossover variants - they are one family. Grid-searching across them and picking a winner is pure data-mining under this result (directly relevant after the 378-cell KDJ exit search lesson).

### Huang, Li, Wang & Zhou (2020), "Time series momentum: Is it there?", Journal of Financial Economics 135(3), 774-794. Verified from published PDF (SMU ink.library open access)
- 方法：Re-examines MOP with the same 55 assets, 1985:01-2015:12. (1) asset-by-asset predictive regressions r_{t+1} = a + b*r_{t-12,t}; (2) pooled regression with wild/pairs bootstrap critical values; (3) compares TSMOM to TSH ('time series history'): buy assets whose HISTORICAL SAMPLE MEAN is positive, sell otherwise - identical vol scaling but zero predictability content.
- 关键结果：Asset-by-asset: only 8/55 assets significant at 10% (3 at 5%); 47/55 have t<1.65; 17/55 (31%) have NEGATIVE slopes; average in-sample R2 = 0.39%; average out-of-sample R2_OS = -0.67%, only 3 assets significantly positive. Pooled: replicates MOP-style t = 4.34, but 5% bootstrap critical values are 12.53 (parametric wild) and 4.83 (nonparametric pairs) - so the pooled evidence is NOT significant; the upward bias comes from unmodeled fixed effects (heterogeneous means) + persistent regressor + heterogeneous vols. TSM vs TSH strategies: virtually identical average and risk-adjusted returns (difference indifferent from zero under all weighting schemes); profits stem mainly from the long legs.
- 数据需求：Same monthly data. The TSH placebo is directly buildable on our names.
- 局限：Does not claim trend strategies lose money - it claims their profit needs no return predictability, only positive unconditional drift plus vol scaling. HUGE for us: on 18 surviving mega-cap tech names the unconditional drift is enormous, so ANY long-flat rule will look good vs cash. Mandatory control per this paper: benchmark every trend rule against (a) same-name buy-and-hold and (b) a TSH-style rule that holds whenever historical mean>0 - if the trend rule cannot beat those, it added nothing. This mirrors the calendar-entry control already used in the KDJ ruling.

### Zakamulin (2014), "The real-life performance of market timing with moving average and time-series momentum rules", Journal of Asset Management 15(4), 261-278. Verified from Nov 2013 SSRN draft PDF (abstract 2242795)
- 方法：Out-of-sample tests robust to the in/out split point: expanding-window rule selection among SMA(k) and MOM(k) on 4 US indices (S&P Composite, DJIA, long-bonds, intermediate bonds) 1926-2012 (OOS 1930-2012), realistic transaction costs; two bootstrap tests (random-walk null vs preserving serial dependence); 1,000-path simulated 'real-life' expected performance.
- 关键结果：Robust OOS vs buy-and-hold on S&P Composite: SMA(k) mean return -20%, std -32%, Sharpe +7% (relative differences); MOM(k) Sharpe +0%. On DJIA both rules have LOWER Sharpe than B&H (-11%, -8%). Only 2/8 index-rule pairs beat passive at all. Reported in-sample claims of 50-100% Sharpe improvement shrink to at best +7-8%. Sell-signal 'failure rate' ~80% (in 80% of sell periods T-bills return less than stocks). Superior performance is confined to a few short bear episodes; timing was inferior for the entire 25-year stretch 1975-1999. Capital growth 1930-2012: B&H ~3x the timing strategy. Timing returns are negatively skewed vs positively skewed B&H (misses the big up-months but keeps whipsaw losses); risk reduction ~30% simply reflects being in cash ~30% of the time. Bootstrap: outperformance fully explained by short-term serial dependence in the data (pval2 0.27-0.86), not by any exploitable extra.
- 数据需求：Nothing we lack; the split-point-robust OOS protocol itself is the import - adopt it for our walk-forward design.
- 局限：Index-level, monthly rules, US only. Its expected-performance estimates assume the historical serial-dependence regime persists. The 80% failure-rate metric is exactly the right prior for exit-rule research: most exits will be 'wrong' and the value is concentrated in rare long bears - so judge exits on tail protection + total return, never on per-exit hit rate (converges with our KDJ round-2 finding that win-rate criteria are gameable).

### D'Souza, Srichanachaichok, Wang & Yao (2016), "The Enduring Effect of Time-Series Momentum on Stock Returns over Nearly 100 Years", SSRN working paper 2720600 (Lancaster/Manchester eprints version: "Time-Series Momentum in Nearly 100 Years of Stock Returns") - numbers below from secondary summaries (CXO/ETF.com/SSRN abstract), not re-verified from the tables
- 方法：TSMOM applied to INDIVIDUAL US stocks: each month long stocks with positive 12-1 past return, short those with negative, hold 1 month; US 1927-2014, international 1975+; also dual momentum (intersection of time-series and cross-sectional).
- 关键结果：Individual-stock TSMOM gross Fama-French 3-factor alpha ~0.70%/month; dual momentum ~1.88%/month gross. Unlike cross-sectional momentum, stock-level TSMOM is profitable after BOTH up- and down-market states and avoids the momentum-crash / January-loss pattern.
- 数据需求：Monthly 12-1 signals per stock - computable on all 18 names for their full histories.
- 局限：Working paper (never verified in a top journal); gross of costs; long-short - the long leg alone is roughly half the effect. Post-2020 sub-sample behavior on mega-cap tech unknown. Still the closest published evidence that per-stock 12-1 sign carries information beyond the index.

### Wilcox & Crittenden (2005), "Does Trend Following Work on Stocks?", Blackstar Funds white paper; modern update: Zarattini, Pagani & Wilcox (2025), "Does Trend-Following Still Work on Stocks?", SSRN 5084316 (numbers via Concretum Group summary page)
- 方法：Long-only single-stock trend: buy on all-time-high breakout (weekly close basis), exit on ATR-based trailing stop; delisting-inclusive database (24,000+ securities, 22 years in 2005 paper; all liquid US stocks 1950-Nov 2024, 66,000+ trades in the 2025 update).
- 关键结果：2005 paper: per-trade expectancy ~+15.2% over an average 305-calendar-day hold; average winner / average loser = 2.56; profits carried by a small minority of large winners (win rate below 50% - the distribution is strongly right-skewed). 2025 update: fewer than 7% of trades generate the majority of cumulative profits; simulated portfolio 1991-2024: 15.19% CAGR, 6.18%/yr annualized alpha.
- 数据需求：Daily OHLC + a liquidity filter; ATR stops need daily highs/lows (we have them). All-time-high breakouts need full price history (we have decades).
- 局限：Non-peer-reviewed practitioner research by fund principals; the 2025 portfolio result uses their turnover-control overlay. Extreme skew means per-name results are dominated by whether the sample contains a mega-winner - on OUR pre-selected winner universe this style will backtest spectacularly for exactly the wrong (survivorship) reason. Useful mainly as the documented shape of long-only stock-trend P&L: low hit rate, long right tail, 300-day holds.

### Short-term reversal cluster: Jegadeesh (1990), "Evidence of Predictable Behavior of Security Returns", Journal of Finance 45(3); Lehmann (1990), "Fads, Martingales, and Market Efficiency", Quarterly Journal of Economics 105(1), 1-28; de Groot, Huij & Zhou (2012), "Another Look at Trading Costs and Short-Term Reversal Profits", Journal of Banking & Finance 36(2), 371-382
- 方法：Buy last week's/month's losers, short winners; weekly (Lehmann) or monthly (Jegadeesh) rebalance. De Groot et al. re-run on large caps with turnover-limited portfolio construction and realistic cost curves.
- 关键结果：Jegadeesh: ~2%/month gross reversal profits 1934-1987. Lehmann: sizeable next-week reversals of prior-week winners/losers, claimed to survive bid-ask corrections (1962-1986). De Groot et al.: naive reversal is killed by costs (turnover ~ 100%+/week concentrated in small caps), but restricted to large caps with smarter construction still nets 30-50 bps/WEEK.
- 数据需求：Weekly returns; the cost modeling is the hard part and we are assuming zero costs at this stage - which is exactly the assumption reversal results are most sensitive to.
- 局限：Superseded for OUR universe by Medhat & Schmeling (next entry): mega-cap tech names are extreme HIGH-turnover stocks, where the weekly/monthly reversal SIGN FLIPS to continuation. Classic reversal is a small-cap, liquidity-provision premium; long-only, costless-paper reversal on NVDA/TSLA is fighting the documented sign.

### Medhat & Schmeling (2022), "Short-term Momentum", Review of Financial Studies 35(3), 1480-1526. Verified from accepted-version PDF (City Research Online eprint 31278)
- 方法：Double decile sort on last month's return x last month's share turnover, NYSE breakpoints, value-weighted, monthly rebalance; US common non-financial shares Jul 1963-Dec 2018 plus 22 developed markets.
- 关键结果：Winners-minus-losers WITHIN the lowest-turnover decile: -16.9%/yr (strong reversal). Within the HIGHEST-turnover decile: +16.4%/yr (short-term momentum - last month's winners keep winning). Conventional all-stock reversal: only -3.39%/yr. Skipping the last few days of the formation month strengthens STM to +22.0%/yr. STM persists ~12 months, is STRONGEST among the largest / most liquid / most covered stocks, and survives conservative transaction-cost estimates (Novy-Marx-Velikov style).
- 数据需求：Monthly returns + share turnover (volume/shares outstanding - Yahoo volume suffices for a proxy; shares outstanding needs a point-in-time source or a constant approximation).
- 局限：Long-short factor evidence, not a timing rule; long-only capture is roughly the long leg. But the sign message is robust and directly on-point: for high-turnover mega-caps, a 1-month continuation tilt is supported; a weekly-reversal overlay is contradicted by the best evidence.

### Moreira & Muir (2017), "Volatility-Managed Portfolios", Journal of Finance 72(4), 1611-1644. Verified from published PDF (author site)
- 方法：Scale next month's factor exposure by c / (previous month's realized variance) (realized variance from daily returns within the month); no return forecasting at all. Applied to market, value, momentum, profitability, BAB, carry.
- 关键结果：For the market portfolio: annualized alpha 4.9%, appraisal ratio 0.33, and a 25% increase in the buy-and-hold Sharpe ratio (sample 1926-2015). Mechanism: variance is highly forecastable at monthly horizon while expected returns barely move with it, so cutting exposure after high-vol months improves the mean-variance trade-off; the strategy de-risked almost completely in fall 2008 and re-entered as vol receded.
- 数据需求：Daily returns only (realized vol). Our 730 days of hourly bars can sharpen the variance estimate (realized vol from intraday returns) but are NOT needed for the core effect.
- 局限：Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan (JFE 2020, "On the performance of volatility-managed portfolios") show the alphas are fragile out-of-sample and largely undiversifiable in real-time implementations for many factors - though the market/equity case is among the more robust. Vol targeting also mechanically raises turnover.

**对本项目的适用性**：What the literature supports for our exact frozen setup (18 surviving US mega/large-cap tech names + hedging ETFs, full daily history, 730d hourly, long-only/long-flat, daily-to-hourly decisions, zero costs, target >=30% ann. / <=20% MaxDD on a validation window):

(1) WHAT THE FILTER BUYS YOU - drawdown, not return. The entire century of evidence says monthly-frequency long/flat trend filters roughly HALVE equity max drawdown while leaving CAGR within ~+/-1pp of buy-and-hold: Faber S&P 1901-2012 (9.32%->10.18% CAGR, -83.46%->-50.29% MaxDD, 2000-02 DD -16.52% vs -44.73%, 2008 fully sidestepped), HOP century Sharpe 1.00 net (but that includes shorts and 59 markets). Zakamulin's split-robust OOS shows the honest expectation: Sharpe at best +7-8% vs B&H, mean return 9-26% LOWER, ~80% of sell signals 'fail', all outperformance concentrated in a few long bears, and a 25-year stretch (1975-1999) of underperformance. CONCLUSION: the >=30% annualized target cannot come from the trend layer; it can only come from the underlying names' drift. The trend layer's realistic job is to hold the <=20% MaxDD line while giving up as little drift as possible - and even that line is beyond documented monthly rules (best documented: ~-42% in the 1930s, -16.5% in 2000-02 for the index). Hitting <=20% on single volatile tech names (B&H DDs of 60-90%) requires daily-cadence rules (e.g., 200-day with a hysteresis band), per-name inverse-vol sizing (Moreira-Muir: +25% Sharpe, 4.9%/yr alpha at index level), and breadth across the 18 names. Whipsaw is the binding failure mode and zero-cost assumptions do NOT hide it: whipsaw loss is a price-path loss (sell low, rebuy higher). Documented scale: SPY crossed its 200-day SMA 22 times Jan 2022-Mar 2023 (StockCharts/Arthur Hill count); Faber's 1973 timing year (-15.36% vs index -14.69%) is the canonical monthly-scale example. Pre-register sideways windows (2015-16, 2018, Apr-Dec 2022) as explicit whipsaw stress tests.

(2) WHICH SIGNAL FAMILY - one family, one horizon choice. Levine-Pedersen proves SMA/EMA/crossover/TSMOM are the same linear filter family: grid-searching across variants is multiple testing with no expected payoff (the direct lesson of our 378-cell KDJ exit search). Choose ONE horizon pair a priori: 12-1 monthly sign (MOP: equity-index alpha t=3.77; d'Souza: works per-stock, FF3 alpha ~0.70%/month gross) or 10-month SMA / 200-day - they are equivalent. For a faster overlay on high-turnover mega-caps, the evidence supports 1-month CONTINUATION (Medhat-Schmeling STM +16.4%/yr in the top turnover decile, strongest in the largest stocks, survives costs) and CONTRADICTS weekly reversal on these names (reversal lives in low-turnover small caps; de Groot's 30-50bp/week is a liquidity-provision premium we cannot harvest long-only in NVDA/TSLA).

(3) MANDATORY CONTROLS - the Huang et al. lesson. TSMOM profits are statistically indistinguishable from 'hold assets with positive unconditional drift' (TSH). On a universe of pre-selected winners this bias is maximal: any long-flat rule on NVDA/AAPL/MSFT will look brilliant because the names were chosen ex post. Every validation must therefore report the rule's margin over (a) same-name buy-and-hold, (b) a TSH/calendar-exposure control matched on average market exposure (~70% invested for the 10m-SMA per Faber), and (c) same-name vol-targeted-only (no trend) - attribution logic identical to the calendar-entry control already used in the KDJ ruling. Judge on return-based criteria (per prior WORKING_MEMORY ruling), expect sub-50% hit rates with right-skewed trade P&L (Wilcox-Crittenden: expectancy +15.2%/trade, 305-day holds, <7% of trades carrying the P&L in the 2025 update), and evaluate exits on tail protection, not per-exit correctness (Zakamulin's 80% failure rate is normal and fine).

(4) DATA-BUDGET REALISM. All load-bearing effects live at monthly/daily frequency, which our decades of daily bars fully support - run the search window pre-2018 and validate 2018+ exactly as in the KDJ rounds. 730 days of hourly bars is ONE macro regime and cannot statistically validate an hourly regime layer (at 12-1 monthly logic that's ~24 non-overlapping observations; at daily-trend logic perhaps 3-5 independent regime episodes) - be direct: any hourly-layer 'validation' on 730 days is anecdote. The defensible uses of hourly data are (i) sharpening realized-vol estimates for the Moreira-Muir-style sizing layer (vol estimation needs short windows, and the layer itself can be validated over decades using daily squared returns as the fallback estimator) and (ii) execution timing of signals generated at daily cadence. The 60/30-day 5-min/1-min stubs support neither validation nor the documented intraday-momentum effect (Gao-Han-Li-Zhou, JFE 2018, needs years of intraday bars).

(5) HONEST VERDICT ON THE 30%/20% TARGET. No published long-only equity trend result achieves 30% annualized with <=20% MaxDD at the index level; per-stock backtests on our universe that do achieve it will owe the 30% to survivorship drift, not to timing skill. Recommended pre-registration: relative criteria - MaxDD reduction >= one-third vs same-name B&H, CAGR retention >= 80% of B&H, positive margin over the TSH/calendar control, bounded whipsaw loss in the pre-registered sideways windows - with the absolute 30%/20% pair reported as an outcome, not used as the pass/fail gate. If the absolute pair must stay, state up front that passing it will be driven by universe drift and cannot be read as strategy evidence transferable to any future universe.

### Lou, Polk, Skouras (2019), "A tug of war: Overnight versus intraday expected returns", Journal of Financial Economics 134(1), 192-213
- 方法：Decompose CRSP close-to-close returns into overnight (close_{t-1} -> open_t, dividends assigned to overnight) and intraday (open->close) components, aggregated to monthly; open prices from CRSP, robustness with first-half-hour VWAP. Microcaps excluded (price<$5, bottom NYSE size quintile). Value-weight decile long-short portfolios for 14 characteristics; CAPM/3-factor alphas; sample 1993-2013.
- 关键结果：Market (VW CRSP): overnight mean +0.55%/mo (t=3.62) vs intraday +0.38%/mo (t=1.87) => ~59% of the equity premium accrues overnight. Persistence: overnight-winner-minus-loser decile has overnight 3F alpha +3.47%/mo (t=16.83) and intraday alpha -3.02%/mo (t=-9.74); intraday-sorted mirror: intraday alpha +2.41%/mo (t=7.70), overnight -1.77%/mo (t=-7.89); effects last up to 60 months. All momentum-type premia are overnight: MOM overnight CAPM alpha +0.98%/mo (t=3.84) vs intraday -0.02% (t=-0.06); overnight MOM Sharpe 0.77 vs 0.31 close-to-close; SUE +0.56%/mo overnight (t=3.20); INDMOM +1.07%/mo overnight (t=6.47) with intraday -0.63% (t=-2.03); STR overnight +0.93% (t=4.28). Conversely the 'risky/glamour' side earns overnight: BETA strategy (long low-beta, short high-beta) overnight alpha -0.49%/mo (t=-2.17), i.e., HIGH-beta beats low-beta overnight by ~0.5%/mo, while intraday +0.70% (t=2.40); IVOL strategy overnight -1.46%/mo (t=-5.23), i.e., high-idio-vol (tech-like) stocks earn ~1.5%/mo more overnight, intraday +2.48% (t=6.21). No industry-level tech split is published, but high-beta/high-IVOL proxies for mega-cap tech load positive overnight, negative intraday.
- 数据需求：Daily open and close prices (decades) per stock; monthly aggregation. Reproducible with Yahoo adjusted daily OHLC provided opens are reliable. No intraday bars required for the core decomposition.
- 局限：Long-short VW deciles on the full CRSP universe - not portable 1:1 to 18 correlated tech names, and the short leg is unavailable to us. Authors state explicitly that transaction costs 'will make the actual profitability... much less attractive' (capturing overnight requires trading at both the close and the open every day). Sample ends 2013; several follow-ups (see Elm Wealth, NY Fed entries) document waning after ~2015. Yahoo open prices are a known weak field in old history - must QA before trusting the split.

### Gao, Han, Li, Zhou (2018), "Market intraday momentum", Journal of Financial Economics 129(2), 394-414
- 方法：Predict SPY last-half-hour return r13 with the first-half-hour return r1 (measured from prior close) and the twelfth half-hour r12; OLS in-sample plus recursive out-of-sample; sign-based timing strategy in the last half hour. SPY 1993-2013; TAQ quotes for cost analysis.
- 关键结果：In-sample R2 = 1.6% (r1 alone), 2.6% (r1+r12); out-of-sample R2 = 1.4% / 2.0%. Timing strategy eta(r1): +6.67%/yr, SD 6.19%, Sharpe 1.08, success rate 54.37%, vs Always-Long-last-half-hour success 50.42% and daily Buy-and-Hold +6.04%/yr, SD 20.57%, Sharpe 0.29. Certainty-equivalent gain 6.02%/yr (risk aversion 5). Predictability rises with volatility (R2 3.3% high-vol tercile; 5.5% on MCSI news days) and holds in 10 other liquid ETFs (Dow, NASDAQ, Russell 2000, bonds).
- 数据需求：30-minute (or finer) intraday bars over thousands of trading days for the ETF; bid-ask quotes near the close for cost netting.
- 局限：After spread costs (post-decimalization TAQ): return falls from 6.93% to 4.46%/yr; post-2005 subsample 7.96% -> 6.52%/yr after costs (SPY spread ~1.2 bp) - survives only in the single most liquid ETF at institutional-grade spreads. Effect concentrates in the LAST 30 minutes, which hourly bars cannot isolate. One trade per day near the close; retail platforms without near-close execution cannot replicate.

### Baltussen, Da, Lammers, Martens (2021), "Hedging demand and market intraday momentum", Journal of Financial Economics 142(1), 377-403
- 方法：Same last-half-hour prediction extended to 62 futures (17 equity-index, 16 bond, 21 commodity, 8 currency), Dec 1974 - May 2020; pooled regressions of rLH on rROD (close_{t-1} to 30 min before close); sign-timing strategies per asset class (1/N). Mechanism: gamma hedging by option market makers and leveraged-ETF rebalancing (NGE proxy from S&P 500 options; LETF AUM x leverage).
- 关键结果：rROD slope for equity futures 2.97 (x100, t=5.02 cluster-robust); pooled effect positive in all four asset classes. Equity-index timing strategy eta(rROD): +6.86%/yr on notional, SD 3.96%, Sharpe 1.73, success 0.55 (vs Always-Long last half hour +0.44%/yr, Sharpe 0.11; Buy-and-Hold +8.76%/yr, Sharpe 0.51). Asset-class Sharpe range 0.87-1.73. Robust across decades 1974-2020 and strong Feb-May 2020. Intraday momentum stronger when market-maker gamma exposure more negative; reverts over following days.
- 数据需求：Intraday futures bars (30-min resolution) across decades; for the mechanism, option open-interest/gamma and LETF AUM data.
- 局限：Explicitly gross of costs: 'the strategy as presented might not be exploitable to many investors after accounting for transaction costs'; positive net Sharpe survives only at ~1-tick cost in S&P futures ('advanced investors'). Effect lives in the last 30 minutes; requires near-close execution in listed futures - not accessible in a UK retail long-only stock/ETF account.

### Bogousslavsky (2021), "The cross-section of intraday and overnight returns", Journal of Financial Economics 141(1), 172-194
- 方法：~30-year sample of US stock intraday returns split into half-hour intervals plus overnight; tracks when each anomaly's premium accrues over the trading day; mechanism: higher overnight margin requirements and lending fees push arbitrageurs to cut positions before the close.
- 关键结果：Anomalies accrue 'in radically different ways' across the day: a composite mispricing factor earns positive returns through most of the day but performs poorly in the last 30 minutes (pattern strengthens in the second half of the sample); size and illiquidity premia are realized almost entirely in the last 30 minutes of trading; momentum-type profits accrue overnight (consistent with LPS). Small-stock turnover spikes at the close. (Precise per-interval bps are in paywalled tables; direction and interval timing are as published in the JFE abstract/highlights.)
- 数据需求：Half-hour interval returns for the cross-section of US stocks over decades (TAQ-grade); overnight returns from daily open/close.
- 局限：Cross-sectional long-short construction on the full universe - not replicable with 18 tech names, no shorting. End-of-day interval effects need 30-min bars; our 5-min history (60 days) and hourly history (730 days) cannot validate them. Companion RFS piece (Bogousslavsky-Fos-Rossi, 'What Drives Momentum and Reversal? Evidence from Day and Night Signals') shows day vs night signals carry different information - useful concept for daily-frequency signal design.

### Hendershott, Livdan, Roesch (2020), "Asset pricing: A tale of night and day", Journal of Financial Economics 138(3), 635-662
- 方法：Beta-decile portfolios and Fama-MacBeth regressions of day (open-close) and night (close-open) returns on rolling 12-month betas; US stocks 1992-2016; 39 non-US countries 1990-2014; TAQ 1993-2016 for around-the-clock figure.
- 关键结果：Security market line is POSITIVE at night and NEGATIVE during the day: +1 beta => +14 bps average night return and -15 bps day return (beta-decile portfolios, R2 96.2% night / 92.2% day). Highest-beta decile: night +20 bps/day, day -8 bps/day. Fama-MacBeth VW: night slope +6.4 bps (t=7.77), day slope -7.7 bps (t=-5.52); EW: +12.1 (t~8.1) vs -13.5 (t=-8.68); night-minus-day implied premium 14.1 bps VW / 25.6 bps EW per day. Holds internationally, for industry and BM portfolios, and for individual stocks. Direct implication: high-beta mega-cap tech earns its premium overnight and loses intraday.
- 数据需求：Daily open and close prices plus rolling betas - fully computable from Yahoo adjusted daily OHLCV with decades of history.
- 局限：Long-short 'betting on beta at night' strategies in the paper trade twice daily; the authors note implementation costs; the anomaly is strongest in the cross-section (we hold only the high-beta side, which is the favorable side overnight). Sample ends 2016; recent-regime stability must be re-verified on own data.

### Boyarchenko, Larsen, Whelan (2023), "The Overnight Drift", Review of Financial Studies 36(9), 3502-3547 (NY Fed Staff Report 917)
- 方法：24h E-mini S&P 500 futures 5-minute returns, 1998-2020 (strategies from 2004); hour-by-hour mean returns with bootstrap/multiple-testing corrections; inventory-risk model: end-of-day order imbalance resolves as overseas traders arrive; DST natural experiments (Tokyo, London opens).
- 关键结果：Largest positive returns 2:00-3:00 am ET (European open): +1.48 bps/day = 3.7%/yr annualized, t high (survives Bonferroni). Since 2004: close-to-close ES +8.9%/yr (Sharpe 0.42), split close-to-open +4.6%/yr vs open-to-close +4.2%/yr; the 2:00-3:00 slice alone +3.8%/yr with Sharpe 1.1 pre-cost. Post bid-ask: simple OD Sharpe -0.5 (1:30-3:30 variant +0.3); conditional buy-the-dip variant (trade only after negative close RSV, ~50% of days) keeps Sharpe 1.1 net.
- 数据需求：24-hour futures data at 5-minute resolution; closing order-imbalance (RSV) measures.
- 局限：Untradeable for us: requires round-the-clock index futures. And the effect died: NY Fed Liberty Street Economics follow-up ("The Disappearing Overnight Drift", July 2026) reports the 2-3 am window, which was >60% of the contract's 5.9%/yr close-to-close return in 1998-2020, has averaged ~zero over 2021-2025; closing imbalance dispersion fell 6.5% -> 2.9%. Prime example of an overnight effect that did not survive its regime.

### Knuteson (2019-2022), "Celebrating Three Decades of Worldwide Stock Market Manipulation" (arXiv:1912.01708), "Strikingly Suspicious Overnight and Intraday Returns" (arXiv:2010.01727), "They Still Haven't Told You" (arXiv:2201.00223) - unrefereed arXiv series
- 方法：Cumulate overnight (close->open) and intraday (open->close) returns separately for 21 major indices/ETFs, ~1990-2021, using publicly available Yahoo Finance data (code published); argues mundane explanations fail and proposes that large players expanding positions in thin morning liquidity and contracting at the close ('mark-to-market pump') explain the split.
- 关键结果：TSX 60 over two decades: overnight +1,062% cumulative vs intraday -67% (exact quote). US panel label (Fig. 1, ~1990-Oct 2020): overnight ~+1,288% vs intraday ~-8%. NASDAQ Composite 1993-01-29 to 2018-10-31 (from his correspondence materials): overnight +2,998% vs intraday -66% - i.e., ~+14.3%/yr overnight vs ~-4.1%/yr intraday annualized (my derivation) for the tech-heavy index, the largest split among US indices. Notes the S&P/NASDAQ pattern visibly changed after ~2008. Cites Cooper, Cliff, Gulen (2008) that removing earnings-announcement days does not change the overnight/intraday split.
- 数据需求：Only daily OHLC from Yahoo - exactly our frozen data. His code reproduces the charts from adjusted opens/closes.
- 局限：Not peer-reviewed; the manipulation hypothesis is not accepted in the literature - refereed explanations include dealer inventory risk (Boyarchenko et al.), short-sale constraints and retail order timing, and opening-auction microstructure. Key practical critique (applies to us): the split is measured at open/close prints where spreads are widest at the open, so measured overnight returns overstate capturable returns. His numbers are index-level price returns, gross of everything.

### Akbas, Boehmer, Jiang, Koch (2022), "Overnight returns, daytime reversals, and future stock returns", Journal of Financial Economics 145(3), 850-875
- 方法：Count, per stock per month, the frequency of days with positive overnight return followed by negative intraday reversal ('daily tug of war' intensity); sort cross-section on this frequency; US stocks.
- 关键结果：Higher tug-of-war intensity (noise-trader overnight pressure being over-corrected by daytime arbitrageurs) predicts HIGHER future cross-sectional returns; the effect is distinct from the level of overnight returns. (Published as a robust cross-sectional predictor; magnitudes are decile-spread alphas in the paper's tables.)
- 数据需求：Daily open and close only - computable from our full-history Yahoo daily OHLC per ticker.
- 局限：Cross-sectional signal built on thousands of stocks; with 18 names it degrades to a weak ranking tilt. Long-short in the paper; we can only use the long side. Treat as a candidate daily-frequency feature, not validated alpha.

### Baltussen, Da, Soebhag (2024/2025), "End-of-Day Reversal", SSRN 5039009 (working paper; EFMA 2024)
- 方法：Individual US stocks: return in the last 30 minutes tends to REVERSE the rest-of-day move for the cross-section of stocks (opposite of the index-level intraday momentum), linked to late-day liquidity provision and retail/institutional flow.
- 关键结果：Documented economically significant end-of-day reversal in single stocks (long-short spreads with high t-stats in the working paper), i.e., the index-level last-half-hour momentum of Gao et al./Baltussen et al. does NOT carry to single names - single names revert at the close.
- 数据需求：30-minute or finer bars on single stocks over many years.
- 局限：Working paper, not yet in a journal - lower evidential weight. Directly warns against applying index-level intraday momentum logic to our 18 single stocks. Untestable on our 60 days of 5-minute data.

### Haghani, White et al. (2023), "Night Moves: Is the Overnight Drift the Grandmother of All Market Anomalies?", Elm Wealth research note (practitioner replication/critique)
- 方法：Replicate overnight-vs-intraday split for S&P 500 1995-2022 and a long-overnight/short-intraday portfolio; stress-test with realistic frictions.
- 关键结果：Long-short (long overnight, short intraday) 1995-2022: ~38%/yr GROSS, t~17. But: 1 bp round-trip cost cuts ~5%/yr; trading 1% of daily volume => ~40 bp round-trip impact, which annihilates the strategy (~200%/yr drag at daily twice-trading); early-1990s commissions (10 bp/trade) would have been a ~100%/yr drag. Five-year rolling returns suggest the effect has been waning in the last 5-10 years, following the 2008-2015 wave of papers.
- 数据需求：Daily open/close for SPY/S&P only.
- 局限：Not peer-reviewed, but the cost arithmetic is the clearest published statement of why twice-daily trading kills gross overnight premia; corroborates NY Fed evidence of post-2021 decay.

**对本项目的适用性**：FEASIBLE NOW, on frozen data. (1) Core decomposition needs only daily OHLC, which we have for decades: compute per-ticker overnight (adjOpen_t/adjClose_{t-1}-1) and intraday (adjClose_t/adjOpen_t-1) series for all 18 stocks + ETFs over full history. This replicates LPS/Hendershott/Knuteson-style splits without any intraday bars. MANDATORY DATA QA FIRST: Yahoo opens are the weakest field in old history (stale/zero opens, open==prior close artifacts); validate daily opens against hourly-bar first-bar opens on the 730-day overlap and against a second source before trusting pre-2000 splits; dividends sit in the overnight leg by construction (matches LPS convention). (2) Expected magnitudes to prereg against: index level overnight premium is single-digit annualized (LPS market overnight 0.55%/mo vs 0.38%/mo intraday 1993-2013; ES close-to-open 4.6%/yr vs open-to-close 4.2%/yr 2004-2020); tech/high-beta is where the split is biggest (NASDAQ overnight ~+14%/yr vs intraday ~-4%/yr 1993-2018; +14 bps/night per unit beta, highest-beta decile night +20 bps vs day -8 bps). So for NVDA/TSLA/AMD-type names, expect most of the drift overnight - useful as a REGIME/STRUCTURE feature, but a long-only overnight-hold sleeve alone does not reach the 30% annualized target; frame it as one layer, not the engine. (3) Stability is the top risk: NY Fed shows the futures overnight drift flat since 2021; Elm shows waning post-2015; Knuteson notes a break after 2008 - so preregister a subperiod split (e.g., pre/post-2015 and post-2021) and require the split to hold in the recent window before using it in the regime layer. (4) With 730 days of hourly bars (~500 trading days) we CAN build coarse intraday-momentum proxies (first-hour return conditioning rest-of-day or last-hour exposure in QQQ/SPY) but with limited power: published R2 is 1.4-2.6% at daily frequency over ~5,000 days; 500 days cannot reject zero at those magnitudes, and the effect concentrates in the last 30 minutes, which hourly bars cannot isolate (and Baltussen-Da-Soebhag show single names REVERSE at the close, so do not apply index intraday momentum to the 18 stocks). Use hourly first-hour/overnight returns as conditioning features for the regime layer, not as a standalone validated signal. 60 days of 5-min and 30 days of 1-min bars are useless for validation - descriptive only. (5) NOT feasible: 2-3 am ET overnight-drift timing (needs 24h futures; effect dead post-2021), cross-sectional long-short decile replication (18 names, no shorting; PSQ/SH/SQQQ are only crude hedge proxies with daily-rebalance decay), last-30-minute strategies. (6) Cost flag (we assume zero costs now, but this decides survival later): capturing overnight means trading at BOTH the close and the open every day; published net results - Gao SPY timing survives at ~1.2 bp spreads (7.96%->6.52%/yr post-2005), Boyarchenko OD Sharpe flips 1.1 -> -0.5 after bid-ask, Elm: 1 bp round trip ~ -5%/yr, 10 bp ~ -100%/yr on twice-daily long-short. Trading 212 per-trade frictions (FX conversion fee on USD instruments, no MOC/LOO auction order types) are UNVERIFIED here and must be pulled from the official T212 fee schedule before any post-cost ruling; at retail-level frictions of 10+ bp/round-trip a daily overnight-capture strategy is dead on arrival, so any design should switch exposure at most a few times per month or use overnight/intraday spread (LPS TugOfWar-style smoothed overnight-minus-intraday component) as a low-turnover regime input to the existing signal layer.

## rigor

### White, H. (2000), "A Reality Check for Data Snooping", Econometrica 68(5), 1097-1126. Flagship applications: Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999), "Data-Snooping, Technical Trading Rule Performance, and the Bootstrap", Journal of Finance 54(5), 1647-1691; Sullivan, Timmermann & White (2001), "Dangers of Data Mining: The Case of Calendar Effects in Stock Returns", Journal of Econometrics 105(1), 249-286.
- 方法：Tests H0: max_k E[f_k] <= 0, i.e. 'the best of m rules does not beat the benchmark', accounting for the FULL search universe. Procedure: (1) f_{k,t} = per-period performance of rule k minus benchmark at t (for a long/flat rule with signal s in {0,1} decided at t-1 vs buy-and-hold: f_{k,t} = s_{k,t-1}*r_t - r_t; log-form log(1+y_t*S_k) - log(1+y_t*S_0) in White/STW). (2) fbar_k = (1/n) sum_t f_{k,t}. (3) Statistic V = max_k sqrt(n)*fbar_k. (4) Stationary-bootstrap resample the t-index B times (jointly across all k, preserving cross-rule dependence); for each b: V*_b = max_k sqrt(n)*(fbar*_{k,b} - fbar_k). (5) RC p-value = #{b: V*_b >= V}/B. Every rule is recentered to 0 (least favorable configuration, LFC). No studentization in the original.
- 关键结果：STW 1999 (7,846 technical rules, DJIA daily 1897-1996): best rule (5-day moving average) annualized return 17.2%, nominal p = 0.000 AND RC p = 0.000 over the 100-year sample -- but out-of-sample 1987-1996 the full-universe best rule earned 14.41%/yr with nominal p = 0.004 vs RC p = 0.341, and on S&P 500 futures 1984-1996 earned 9.43%/yr with nominal p = 0.042 vs RC p = 0.908. STW 2001 (9,452 calendar rules, 1897-1996): best rule 8.66%/yr vs 4.63% benchmark, nominal p = 0.000 vs RC p = 0.243. I.e. accounting for the search inflates p-values from 'discovery' to 'nothing there' -- exactly the regime this project operates in.
- 数据需求：The complete per-period performance-differential series for EVERY rule ever tried (a synchronous n x m matrix), not just survivors; a defensible benchmark; n large relative to the dependence length. B >= 1,000 bootstrap replications.
- 局限：Two documented defects: (a) conservative, because the LFC recenters even hopeless rules to the null boundary -- Hansen (2005) proved the null distribution asymptotically depends only on the zero-mean rules, so RC over-covers; (b) manipulable: ADDING known-bad rules to the universe mechanically inflates the RC p-value of a genuinely good rule (Hansen 2005 simulations), so an honest RC requires an honestly-frozen universe. Not studentized, so high-variance rules dominate the max. Superseded by SPA for our use; keep RC only as the historical baseline.

### Hansen, P. R. (2005), "A Test for Superior Predictive Ability", Journal of Business & Economic Statistics 23(4), 365-380. Power comparison and stepwise extension: Hsu, Hsu & Kuan (2010), Journal of Empirical Finance 17(3), 471-484. Flagship application: Hansen & Lunde (2005), Journal of Applied Econometrics 20(7), 873-889.
- 方法：Same H0 as RC but studentized and with a sample-dependent null that avoids the LFC. IMPLEMENTABLE PSEUDOCODE (d_{k,t} = model k performance minus benchmark, k=1..m, t=1..n):
1. dbar_k = mean_t(d_{k,t}).
2. HAC variance consistent with the stationary bootstrap (Hansen eq. for omega^2, kernel from Politis-Romano): Gamma_{k,j} = (1/n)*sum_{t=j+1..n}(d_{k,t}-dbar_k)*(d_{k,t-j}-dbar_k); kappa(j) = ((n-j)/n)*(1-q)^j + (j/n)*(1-q)^(n-j) where q = 1/(mean block length b); omega_k^2 = Gamma_{k,0} + 2*sum_{j=1..n-1} kappa(j)*Gamma_{k,j}.
3. Test statistic: T_SPA = max( max_k sqrt(n)*dbar_k/omega_k , 0 ).
4. Recentering vector (this is the whole point). Threshold A_k = -omega_k*sqrt(2*log(log(n))). Three variants:
   consistent: mu_k = dbar_k if sqrt(n)*dbar_k <= A_k, else 0  (significantly bad rules stay at their bad mean; the rest sit on the null boundary);
   upper:      mu_k = 0 for all k (= RC-like, most conservative);
   lower:      mu_k = min(dbar_k, 0) (least conservative).
5. Stationary bootstrap (Politis-Romano 1994), B >= 1,000: draw index path tau_1..tau_n: tau_1 ~ U{1..n}; for t>1, with prob (1-q) tau_t = tau_{t-1}+1 (wrap n->1), with prob q tau_t ~ U{1..n}. Resample rows JOINTLY across k. dbar*_{k,b} = mean_t d_{k,tau_t}.
6. T*_b = max( max_k sqrt(n)*(dbar*_{k,b} - dbar_k + mu_k)/omega_k , 0 ).
7. p = #{b: T*_b >= T_SPA}/B. Report p_lower <= p_consistent <= p_upper; decide on p_consistent at alpha = 0.05.
Why it dominates RC: (i) studentization stops high-variance junk rules from driving the max; (ii) recentering removes the LFC so poor rules cannot inflate the p-value -- Hansen shows RC's null distribution should depend only on the binding (zero-mean) rules, and SPA implements exactly that.
- 关键结果：Hansen's simulations: RC loses power 'dramatically' as poor models are added; SPA is nearly unaffected. Hsu-Hsu-Kuan stepwise simulations (n=1000, B=500, Q=0.9, m=9,000 with unequal groups): average rejection rate 81.4% for Step-SPA vs 65.4% for Step-RC (16-point power gap); FWER of both controlled at 5%. Hansen-Lunde applied SPA to 330 volatility models vs GARCH(1,1) (the standard empirical template for 'does anything beat the benchmark').
- 数据需求：Same n x m differential matrix as RC. Block parameter q: set b via Politis-White 2004 automatic selection on representative d_k series, then q = 1/b; rerun at b/2 and 2b as robustness. n = 3,500 (hourly window) or 5,000+ (daily, 20y) is comfortable; m up to tens of thousands is fine (STW/HHK scale).
- 局限：p-value depends on q/block length -- always report the sensitivity; the 'lower' p-value can be anti-conservative (report all three, decide on consistent); size distortions of hybrid/extreme configurations documented in later literature (e.g. arXiv:2008.02318). Studentization uses full-sample omega_k in both statistic and bootstrap (Hsu et al. footnote 6); re-estimating omega per resample is the Romano-Wolf flavor and also valid. Like RC it answers only 'does the BEST rule beat the benchmark' -- for WHICH rules, use the stepwise versions (see Romano-Wolf entry).

### Politis, D. N. & Romano, J. P. (1994), "The Stationary Bootstrap", Journal of the American Statistical Association 89(428), 1303-1313; Politis, D. N. & White, H. (2004), "Automatic Block-Length Selection for the Dependent Bootstrap", Econometric Reviews 23(1), 53-70; Patton, Politis & White (2009), Correction, Econometric Reviews 28(4), 372-375.
- 方法：Stationary bootstrap (SB): resample blocks of geometric random length (continue prob 1-q, mean length b = 1/q), wrapping circularly; the resampled series is strictly stationary and preserves weak dependence -- this is the resampling engine inside RC, SPA and Romano-Wolf. Automatic block length (PW 2004 as CORRECTED by PPW 2009): b_opt_SB = (2*Ghat^2 / Dhat_SB)^(1/3) * n^(1/3), with Dhat_SB = 2*ghat(0)^2 (the 2009 correction; PW 2004's original D_SB formula was wrong via Lahiri 1999, fixed after Nordman 2008). Ghat = sum_{k=-M..M} lambda(k/M)*|k|*Rhat(k); ghat(0) = sum_{k=-M..M} lambda(k/M)*Rhat(k); Rhat(k) = sample autocovariance; flat-top window lambda(t) = 1 for |t|<=1/2, 2(1-|t|) for 1/2<|t|<=1, 0 otherwise. Bandwidth rule (Politis 2001, restated in PW 2004 Sec. 3.2 footnote): mhat = smallest integer such that |rho_hat(mhat+k)| < 2*sqrt(log10(n)/n) for k = 1..K_n consecutive lags, K_n = max(5, sqrt(log10(n))); then M = 2*mhat. For circular/moving blocks: b_opt_CB = (2*Ghat^2/Dhat_CB)^(1/3)*n^(1/3) with Dhat_CB = (4/3)*ghat(0)^2. Patton's corrected MATLAB (and the R package np / blocklength ports) implement exactly this.
- 关键结果：PPW 2009 corrected tables: for AR(1) rho=0.7, n=200: b_opt_SB = 11.47 (n=800: 18.20); rho=0.1, n=200: b_opt_SB = 2.01; the automatic estimator lands between 90% and 110% of true optimum on average, RMSE roughly halving from n=200 to n=800. Asymptotic relative efficiency ARE_CB/SB = (2/3)^(2/3) = 0.763 (circular blocks mildly more efficient for variance estimation, but SB's b-estimate is more accurate and SB is least sensitive to misspecified b).
- 数据需求：Only the series being resampled (for SPA: the loss-differential series; pick a representative one or the benchmark-differential of the current best rule). n >= a few hundred. For our daily 20y series n>5,000, hourly n around 3,500 -- both fine.
- 局限：Use the CORRECTED D_SB = 2*g(0)^2 -- pre-2009 code and several textbook reproductions carry the wrong constant. Block length tuned for mean-type statistics; Sharpe-type studentized statistics with volatility clustering need blocks long enough for second-moment dependence -- for hourly equity bars run robustness at b = 7 (one session) regardless of what the autocorrelation-based rule returns, since return autocorrelation is near zero but vol clustering is not.

### Bailey, D. H. & Lopez de Prado, M. (2014), "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio: Correcting for Selection Bias, Backtest Overfitting and Non-Normality", Journal of Portfolio Management 40(5), 94-107 (SSRN 2460551). PSR from: Bailey & Lopez de Prado (2012), "The Sharpe Ratio Efficient Frontier", Journal of Risk 15(2), 3-44.
- 方法：EXACT FORMULAS (verified against the paper PDF; the worked example reproduces to 4 decimals). All SRs NON-ANNUALIZED, same periodicity as the T observations -- unit mixing is the #1 implementation bug.
(A) Expected max SR under H0 (paper eq. 1/5, via EVT + Euler-Mascheroni gamma = 0.5772156649):
    E[max_N] = (1-gamma)*Zinv(1 - 1/N) + gamma*Zinv(1 - 1/(N*e))   [max of N iid standard normals; error -> 0 as N grows]
    SR0 = sqrt(V[{SR_n}]) * E[max_N]   (null: true SR = 0; V[{SR_n}] = variance of the N trials' estimated SRs)
(B) PSR (per-period): PSR(SR*) = Z[ (SRhat - SR*)*sqrt(T-1) / sqrt(1 - g3*SRhat + ((g4-1)/4)*SRhat^2) ], g3 = skewness, g4 = kurtosis (non-excess; Normal g4 = 3). Denominator is the Mertens(2002)/Lo(2002) non-normality-adjusted SE of the SR estimator.
(C) DSR = PSR(SR0). Accept at 95% confidence iff DSR >= 0.95.
(D) Effective independent trials when the M actual trials are correlated (paper eq. 8-9, Appendix 3): rho_bar = (2*sum_{i<j} rho_ij)/(M*(M-1)) from the M x M correlation matrix of trial returns; N_eff = rho_bar + (1 - rho_bar)*M; use N_eff as N in (A).
PSEUDOCODE:
  1. maintain trial ledger: all M trials' per-period return series
  2. rho_bar = avg off-diagonal corr of trial returns; N = rho_bar + (1-rho_bar)*M
  3. V = var over trials of per-period SR_n estimates
  4. SR0 = sqrt(V) * ((1-g)*norminv(1-1/N) + g*norminv(1-1/(N*exp(1))))
  5. r = selected strategy per-period returns; T = len(r); SRhat = mean(r)/std(r); g3 = skew(r); g4 = kurt(r)
  6. DSR = normcdf((SRhat-SR0)*sqrt(T-1)/sqrt(1 - g3*SRhat + (g4-1)/4*SRhat^2))
  7. pass iff DSR >= 0.95 (also report PSR(0) and MinTRL if needed).
- 关键结果：Paper's worked example, reproduced exactly in this session: annualized SR 2.5 over 5 years of daily data (T = 1,250), N = 100 trials, V[{SR_n}] = 0.5 (annualized units, /250 for daily), skew = -3, kurtosis = 10 => SR0 = 0.1132 daily (about 1.79 annualized) and DSR = 0.9004 < 0.95: an apparent SR 2.5 five-year backtest is NOT significant at 95% once 100 trials and fat tails are admitted. Same discovery would have passed with N = 46 trials (DSR 0.9505), or with N = 88 had returns been Normal. Reference noise table (computed from eq. 1, E[max SR] of pure noise, annualized): on a 2.0-year window best-of-10 = 1.11, best-of-50 = 1.61, best-of-100 = 1.79, best-of-1000 = 2.30; on a 20-year window best-of-100 = 0.57, best-of-1000 = 0.73.
- 数据需求：The five inputs beyond the backtest itself: N (or M + trial correlation matrix), V[{SR_n}], T, skew, kurtosis. This REQUIRES logging every trial's return series -- retrofitting N from memory is exactly the failure mode the method exists to prevent.
- 局限：E[max_N] assumes iid Normal trial SRs; correlated trials must go through N_eff (with our 18 highly-correlated tech names plus nested parameter grids, expect N_eff << M -- compute it, do not guess). Understating N (forgotten trials) silently un-deflates: the ledger is the integrity mechanism, the formula cannot self-police. V[{SR_n}] estimated from a trial family that mixes strategy classes is meaningless -- compute per strategy class. Harvey-Liu (JPM 2015 'Backtesting') offer the alternative haircut-Sharpe route based on their multiple-testing framework; DSR and HLZ thresholds are complementary (the DSR paper itself recommends computing DSR against the HL threshold too).

### Bailey, D. H., Borwein, J., Lopez de Prado, M. & Zhu, Q. J. (2017), "The Probability of Backtest Overfitting", Journal of Computational Finance 20(4), 39-69 (SSRN 2326253, first posted 2013).
- 方法：CSCV (combinatorially symmetric cross-validation), verbatim from Algorithm 2.3: (1) Build matrix M of order T x N: column n = the full P&L/return series of trial n, rows synchronous across all N trials (aggregate to a common index if frequencies differ). (2) Partition rows into S disjoint contiguous submatrices of equal size (S even), each (T/S) x N. (3) Form ALL combinations of the S blocks taken S/2 at a time: C(S, S/2) splits (S = 16 gives 12,870; the paper misprints '12,780'). (4) For each combination c: training set J = the chosen S/2 blocks re-joined in original order (T/2 x N); test set Jbar = complement; compute the performance metric (e.g. SR) for all N columns on J and on Jbar; let n* = argmax on J (the IS winner); let rbar = rank of column n* among the N columns ON Jbar; relative rank omega_c = rbar/(N+1); logit lambda_c = ln(omega_c/(1-omega_c)). (5) PBO = fraction of the C(S,S/2) splits with lambda_c <= 0, i.e. the probability that the IS-chosen configuration performs BELOW MEDIAN out of sample. Companion diagnostics from the same splits: performance-degradation regression (OOS SR of n* regressed on IS SR of n*: slope and intercept), probability of OOS loss Prob[R_n* < 0], and first/second-order stochastic dominance of the selected-strategy OOS distribution vs the unconditional one.
- 关键结果：Framework definition: overfit selection process := PBO where the IS-best underperforms the OOS median. Paper's illustrative overfit seasonal strategy shows PBO = 74%. Degradation regressions on real trial sets typically show negative slope (IS rank anti-predicts OOS rank once overfit). The paper suggests uses such as rejecting models with PBO > 0.05 or using PBO as a Bayesian prior; it does NOT canonize a single cutoff -- the cutoff must be preregistered by the user. S = 16 balances split granularity vs compute (12,870 evaluations of N columns each).
- 数据需求：The FULL T x N trial matrix -- the heaviest data requirement of any method here, and the same ledger DSR needs. Blocks stay contiguous so within-block serial dependence is preserved; T must be large enough that T/S blocks (T/16) still contain meaningful structure: daily 20y gives blocks of about 315 trading days (good); hourly 730d gives blocks of about 225 bars = 32 sessions (acceptable mechanically, weak economically -- one macro regime).
- 局限：(a) Rank-based: measures selection-process degradation, not absolute skill -- a family of uniformly bad strategies can have low PBO; pair with SPA/DSR, never use alone. (b) Gameable in the OPPOSITE direction from RC: padding the trial matrix with junk columns inflates the IS-winner's OOS relative rank and lowers PBO -- the frozen trial ledger is again the countermeasure. (c) Combinatorial splits juxtapose non-adjacent epochs, so regime structure at horizons longer than T/S is scrambled; on a 2-year hourly matrix PBO says nothing about robustness across bull/bear regimes. (d) The train/test symmetry means CSCV estimates the overfit probability of the SELECTION PROCEDURE, conditional on the trial set -- it is not an out-of-sample test of the final strategy.

### Harvey, C. R., Liu, Y. & Zhu, H. (2016), "... and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", Review of Financial Studies 29(1), 5-68 (NBER WP 20592).
- 方法：Catalogue of 316 factors from 313 published works plus 63 selected working papers, 1967-2012; reconstructs the implied multiple-testing hurdle over time under three adjustments applied to the cross-section of published t-statistics: Bonferroni (reject if p <= alpha/M), Holm (order p(1)<=...<=p(M), reject while p(j) <= alpha/(M-j+1)), and Benjamini-Hochberg-Yekutieli FDR (c(M) = sum_{j=1..M} 1/j; find largest k with p(k) <= k*alpha_d/(M*c(M)), reject 1..k; valid under arbitrary dependence). A separate simulation framework backs out the unpublished-factor population and allows test correlation.
- 关键结果：Hurdles as of 2012 (from the paper text): Bonferroni t = 3.78 (projected 4.00 by 2032); Holm t = 3.64 (3.29 had there been only 113 factors -- the hurdle grows with the discovery count); BHY at 1% FDR t = 3.39 (stable after 2010), at 5% FDR t = 2.78 (2.81 by 2032). Correlation-adjusted simulation raises BHY to 3.68 (1%) and 3.18 (5%); the authors' stated minimum threshold is 3.18 and the headline recommendation is t > 3.0 for any newly proposed factor, versus the classical 1.96. Of 296 published significant factors: 158 are false discoveries under Bonferroni, 142 under Holm, 132 under BHY(1%), 80 under BHY(5%) -- 'most claimed research findings in financial economics are likely false'. t = 3.0 on a Sharpe scale: t = SR_ann*sqrt(years), so t >= 3 requires realized annualized SR >= 0.67 over 20 years, 0.95 over 10 years, 1.34 over 5 years, 2.12 over 2 years.
- 数据需求：Only the t-statistic (or SR and sample length) of each candidate plus an honest trial count; no bootstrap. Directly usable as a scalar gate: t = SRhat*sqrt(T_periods) per period, or DSR >= 0.95 as the finer-grained equivalent.
- 局限：Derived for the PUBLISHED cross-sectional factor literature; the multiplicity it corrects is the field's, not your private search -- for an in-house grid search DSR with the logged N is the correct instrument and HLZ t>3.0 is the floor, not the ceiling. The authors themselves note theory-motivated factors merit a lower hurdle and purely empirical ones a higher hurdle. t>3.0 assumes roughly normal test statistics -- with the skew/kurtosis of trading-strategy returns, route through PSR instead of raw t where possible.

### Romano, J. P. & Wolf, M. (2005), "Stepwise Multiple Testing as Formalized Data Snooping", Econometrica 73(4), 1237-1282. Efficient computation: Romano & Wolf (2016), Statistics & Probability Letters 113, 38-40. More powerful successor: Hsu, Hsu & Kuan (2010) Step-SPA, Journal of Empirical Finance 17(3), 471-484.
- 方法：Answers WHICH of m strategies beat the benchmark, controlling FWER (prob of even one false claim) -- RC/SPA only say whether the best one does. StepM procedure: (1) studentized statistics z_k = sqrt(n)*dbar_k/sigma_k (studentization strongly recommended; sigma_k from HAC or bootstrap); (2) bootstrap the JOINT distribution (circular block bootstrap in RW; stationary works too): for each resample compute max over the CURRENT active set of (dbar*_k - dbar_k)/sigma*_k; c1 = (1-alpha) quantile of that max; reject every k with z_k > c1; (3) remove rejected hypotheses, recompute the max-quantile over survivors only (c2 < c1), reject more; (4) iterate until no new rejections. Rejections at every step inherit FWER <= alpha asymptotically because each subsequent max is over a smaller set. Adjusted p-values computable in one pass per RW 2016 (3-line algorithm). Step-SPA = same stepdown but with Hansen's recentering mu_k instead of the LFC, provably more powerful (HHK Theorem 2.3).
- 关键结果：RW prove FWER control under weak dependence assumptions and show studentized StepM attains materially higher 'average power' than single-step RC-type tests; HHK quantify the stack: at m = 9,000 with many poor rules, Step-SPA rejection rate 81.4% vs Step-RC 65.4% at the same controlled 5% FWER, with the gap widening as junk share rises. The stepdown refinement itself adds only a little power over one-step (HHK: 91.9% -> 94.2% at m = 900) -- the big win is recentering + studentization; the stepdown's real value is the per-strategy decision list.
- 数据需求：Same n x m differential matrix as SPA. Compute cost = SPA times the number of steps (usually 2-4). Use whenever more than one configuration will be carried forward to live testing.
- 局限：FWER control at strategy-family scale is stringent; with thousands of correlated configs it rejects few -- that is the honest answer, not a defect. For screening (not certification) an FDR-controlling variant (Benjamini-Hochberg on SPA-consistent per-strategy p-values, or RW's k-FWER/FDP extensions, Annals of Statistics 2007) is the pragmatic middle ground. RW's original uses circular blocks with data-dependent length and per-resample sigma*; HHK's uses stationary bootstrap with fixed Q and full-sample sigma -- both valid, do not mix conventions within one run.

### Bailey, D. H., Borwein, J. M., Lopez de Prado, M. & Zhu, Q. J. (2014), "Pseudo-Mathematics and Financial Charlatanism: The Effects of Backtest Overfitting on Out-of-Sample Performance", Notices of the American Mathematical Society 61(5), 458-471.
- 方法：Minimum Backtest Length: inverts the E[max_N] formula to give the shortest backtest (in years, for annualized SR) that keeps the expected best-of-N pure-noise Sharpe below a target level SRbar: MinBTL ~ ( [(1-gamma)*Zinv(1-1/N) + gamma*Zinv(1-1/(N*e))] / SRbar )^2 < 2*ln(N)/SRbar^2. Companion theorem: for processes with memory (mean reversion), overfit in-sample rule selection produces systematically NEGATIVE out-of-sample expected performance, not merely zero -- overfitting maximizes future loss, because the selected rule is fit to extreme patterns that revert.
- 关键结果：To keep the noise-max below annualized SR 1.0: N = 45 trials already needs about 5 years of data; N = 100 needs 6.4 years; N = 1,000 needs 10.6 years. Inverted for OUR hourly window: with only 2.0 years of data, the expected best-of-N noise Sharpe crosses 1.0 after only about 7-8 trials, and reaches 1.79 by N = 100. E[max_N] grows like sqrt(2*ln N) -- unbounded, so no fixed SR level is safe under unrecorded search.
- 数据需求：Only N and the data span; usable as a back-of-envelope go/no-go before any heavier machinery.
- 局限：iid-normal trials assumption as in DSR (use N_eff for correlated trials); MinBTL is a necessary-length bound, not a sufficiency guarantee; the negative-OOS theorem needs the memory (mean-reversion) property, which holds for most equity series per the authors.

**对本项目的适用性**：Bottom line for our frozen data (18 surviving US tech names + 17 ETFs, Yahoo adjusted daily full history, hourly 730d, 5-min 60d, 1-min 30d; long-only/long-flat; 30%+ annualized with MDD<=20% on a validation window):

1. WHAT IS NOT FEASIBLE -- honest numbers first. The 730-day hourly window is 2.0 years (~500 sessions x 7 bars = ~3,500 bars/ticker). Single-trial t=3.0 (the HLZ floor) on 2 years requires realized annualized SR >= 2.12. Under pure noise, the expected best-of-N annualized SR on a 2-year window is 1.11 (N=10), 1.61 (N=50), 1.79 (N=100), 2.30 (N=1000) -- and our target (30% return at ~20% vol, i.e. SR ~1.5) sits INSIDE best-of-50 noise territory. Conclusion: the 30%/20% target cannot be statistically validated on the hourly window alone by any procedure in this survey; after ~7-8 logged trials the expected noise-max already exceeds SR 1.0 there (MinBTL). The 5-min (0.16y) and 1-min (0.08y) sets are statistically useless for selection at any threshold; restrict them to regime-proxy fidelity checks and later execution/cost modeling -- no selection decision may touch them.

2. WHAT IS FEASIBLE. On 20 years of daily data, t=3 needs SR >= 0.67 while best-of-100 noise is ~0.57 -- genuine discrimination exists. Therefore: put the entire statistical load on the daily layer. Build DAILY proxies of the high-frequency information for the regime layer (Parkinson/Garman-Klass/Rogers-Satchell vol from daily OHLC, overnight-vs-intraday return split, cross-asset stress via TLT/GLD/UUP/GDX/VXX levels and slopes, breadth across the 18 names) so the regime layer is testable over decades; use the hourly 730d only to (a) verify proxy fidelity against true intraday measures and (b) serve as ONE pre-registered, frozen confirmation window for the finished daily-layer model -- pass/fail criteria written into research/prereg/ before the window is ever opened, trial count on that window = 1 by construction.

3. CONCRETE GATE STACK (all parameters implementable from the paper entries above): (i) Trial ledger as mandatory infrastructure: every configuration ever run appends its full per-period return column to matrix M (T x N) with config hash + timestamp; N is cumulative across the whole research program. This single artifact feeds DSR, CSCV and SPA, and is the countermeasure to all three documented gaming modes (junk rules inflate RC p, junk trials deflate PBO, forgotten trials un-deflate DSR). (ii) Family gate vs benchmark: Hansen SPA consistent p <= 0.05 on daily data, studentized, stationary bootstrap B >= 1,000, block length from Politis-White 2004 with the PPW 2009 correction (D_SB = 2g(0)^2), robustness at b/2 and 2b, and b = 7 bars forced as a robustness point on any hourly run; benchmark = buy-and-hold of the SAME instrument (long/flat is a timing overlay; an absolute-return benchmark would launder beta -- and this survivorship-selected universe's beta is enormous). Report p_l/p_c/p_u. (iii) Per-discovery gate: DSR >= 0.95 with N_eff = rho_bar + (1-rho_bar)*M computed from the trial-return correlation matrix (expect N_eff << M: correlated universe, nested grids -- compute, never guess), all SR quantities per-period non-annualized (the worked example above reproduces to 4 decimals as a unit test: N=100, T=1250, V=0.5 ann., skew -3, kurt 10, SR 2.5 ann. => SR0=0.1132 daily, DSR=0.9004). Equivalent scalar floor: t >= 3.0 per HLZ. (iv) Selection gate when carrying multiple configs forward: Romano-Wolf StepM or (preferably) Step-SPA at FWER 5%; if that rejects nothing, fall back to BH-FDR on SPA per-strategy p-values for screening only, never for certification. (v) Process gate: CSCV on the daily M with S=16 (12,870 splits; blocks ~315 trading days on 20y); pre-register the PBO cutoff (0.10 strict / 0.20 tolerant -- practitioner convention, the paper fixes none) plus require non-negative IS->OOS degradation slope. Hourly-matrix CSCV (blocks ~225 bars) is computable but spans a single macro regime -- secondary evidence only.

4. UNIVERSE AND DATA CAVEATS THAT NO STATISTIC FIXES. (a) '18 surviving' is survivorship selection by construction; every long-only decades-long backtest on it is inflated, and RC/SPA/DSR/CSCV correct multiplicity, not survivorship -- frame all absolute-return conclusions as conditional on this universe, and prefer benchmarks built from the same universe (equal-weight B&H) so the bias cancels across arms. (b) The panel is unbalanced: several tickers (PLTR, DELL post-relisting, current VXX series, others) do not have decades -- exact usable start dates are S5-verifiable facts to be read from the frozen files, not assumed; SPA and CSCV need synchronous T x N, so define each experiment's window by common coverage or run per-name. (c) Yahoo hourly bar conventions (7 bars/session, last bar 30 min, adjustment treatment intraday) must be verified from the frozen data before any hourly statistic is computed. (d) Zero-cost is acceptable for signal ordering at this stage, but daily-to-hourly turnover on a GBP retail account will erode a 30% gross target materially -- schedule the cost pass before anything is promoted, outside this survey's scope.

5. DECISION RULE TO PRE-REGISTER NOW: a candidate system is 'validated' only if, on the daily layer, SPA-consistent p <= 0.05 vs same-universe B&H AND DSR >= 0.95 at the ledger's cumulative N_eff AND PBO <= the preregistered cutoff; and then, on the single frozen hourly confirmation window, it meets the preregistered economic bar (>=30% ann., MDD <= 20%) WITHOUT that window ever having been searched. Hitting 30%/20% on the hourly window with DSR < 0.95 is recorded as a FAIL. This is the only ordering of these instruments under which the 730-day hourly history contributes evidence instead of consuming it.

## intraday-feasible

### Gao, Han, Li, Zhou (2018), Market Intraday Momentum, Journal of Financial Economics 129(2), 394-414
- 方法：Predictive regression r13,t = a + b*r1,t + e on SPY, where r1 = first half-hour return measured from PRIOR close (so it embeds the overnight gap) and r13 = last half-hour (15:30-16:00) return; 13 half-hour bars/day. Timing strategy: go long (short) the last half-hour if sign(r1) positive (negative), flat otherwise.
- 关键结果：SPY Feb-1993 to Dec-2013 (~5,200 trading days, TAQ). Scaled slope 6.94, robust t = 4.08, in-sample R2 = 1.6%, out-of-sample R2 = 1.4%; adding r12 (second-to-last half-hour): R2 = 2.6%, OOS 2.0%. R2 rises to 3.3% in the high first-half-hour-volatility tercile, 5.5% on macro-news days, 11.0% on FOMC days. Timing strategy: 6.67%/yr avg return, SD 6.19%, Sharpe 1.08, skew +0.90, success rate 54.37% (vs 50.42% always-long); buy-and-hold Sharpe 0.29. Cross-ETF: in-sample R2 1.16% (DIA) to 8.54% (EEM); OOS R2 0.70% (QQQ) to 6.53% (EEM). Source: working-paper PDF (assets.super.so mirror of SSRN 2440866), sections 2-4 and Tables 1, 8, 12.
- 数据需求：Half-hour bars (or at least prior close + 10:00 price + 15:30 price + close) for one liquid index ETF; 20 years used in the paper. Our Yahoo hourly bars CANNOT form the 9:30-10:00 first half-hour, but Yahoo's hourly grid (9:30, 10:30, ..., 15:30) means the LAST bar is exactly 15:30-16:00, so rLH and the Baltussen-style rest-of-day predictor rROD (prior close to 15:30) ARE computable for ~505 trading days. Bar-timestamp convention must first be verified empirically against the 60-day 5-min data (S5 check).
- 局限：Sample ends 2013; the published effect size (R2 1.6%) is exactly at the edge of what 505 daily observations can detect (expected t = 2.9, power 82% at alpha=5%). Any post-publication decay (documented weakening in US index intraday momentum post-2014 in several replications; QQQ OOS R2 was already only 0.70% in-sample-period) drops power below 40%. Strategy trades the last half-hour only and earns 6.67%/yr - an order of magnitude below the 30%/yr goal even at full published strength. Long-only constraint discards the short leg (~half the signal days).

### Baltussen, Da, Lammers, Martens (2021), Hedging Demand and Market Intraday Momentum, Journal of Financial Economics 142(1), 377-403
- 方法：Same last-half-hour regression on 62 futures (17 equity index, 16 bond, 21 commodity, 8 currency), Dec 1974 - May 2020. Key refinement: the rest-of-day return rROD (prior close to start of last half-hour) is a stronger and more robust predictor of rLH than the first half-hour rONFH. Mechanism: gamma hedging by option market makers and leveraged-ETF rebalancing (both must trade in the direction of the day's move near the close).
- 关键结果：rROD predicts rLH across all asset classes; pooled OOS R2 = 2.88% for the combined model (their Table, Column 3); asset-class-level timing strategies have annualized Sharpe ratios 0.87-1.73 with success rates well above 50%. Predictability reverts over subsequent days. rSLH (second-to-last half-hour) does NOT generalize outside equities. Source: JFE published PDF (www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf), abstract, Section 1, Eq. (5)-(8).
- 数据需求：Only the prior close, the 15:30 price, and the close, per day - fully computable from our 730-day Yahoo hourly bars for QQQ/SPY/IWM and all 18 stocks. This, not Gao's first-half-hour version, is the variant our data supports directly.
- 局限：Futures, not cash ETFs; leveraged-ETF rebalancing flow is concentrated in index products, so mechanism is weakest for single stocks. Effect size at the market level is a few bps per day; a last-half-hour-only long/flat overlay cannot approach 30%/yr. Mechanism-driven (gamma exposure) - effect sign flips when dealer gamma is long, which we cannot observe without options data.

### Zarattini, Barbon, Aziz (2024), Beat the Market: An Effective Intraday Momentum Strategy for S&P 500 ETF (SPY), SSRN 4824172 / Swiss Finance Institute WP
- 方法：Practitioner-style extension: instead of trading only the last 30 minutes, opens intraday trend positions whenever price breaks a 'noise band' around the open (band width = average absolute move at that time-of-day over trailing 14 days), with VWAP-anchored dynamic trailing stops; positions closed at the day's end.
- 关键结果：SPY 1-minute data 2007 - early 2024 (~4,300 days): total return 1,985% net of assumed costs, annualized 19.6%, Sharpe 1.33, versus SPY buy-and-hold Sharpe ~0.6 over the same window. Source: SSRN abstract page 4824172 and Concretum Group summary.
- 数据需求：Full-session 1-minute or 5-minute bars, 15+ years; also live intraday execution capability. We have only 60 days of 5-min / 30 days of 1-min - validation impossible on our frozen dataset; would require a data purchase (any vendor below) plus intraday execution, which exceeds the stated daily-to-hourly decision frequency.
- 局限：Not peer-reviewed; authors run a trading-education/consulting firm (promotional incentive). Time-of-day noise band and trailing stops introduce many researcher degrees of freedom; no independent replication of the net-of-cost numbers. An independent falsification study on MNQ futures (below) finds this class of OHLCV intraday signal does not survive realistic frictions in 2021-2025.

### Limkriangkrai, Chai, Zheng (2023), Market Intraday Momentum: APAC Evidence, Pacific-Basin Finance Journal 80, 102086
- 方法：Direct replication of Gao et al. (2018) regressions (r1 and rn-1 predicting last half-hour) on index ETFs in China, Japan, Hong Kong, Singapore, South Korea, samples 2000/2012 - Apr 2021.
- 关键结果：Effect exists in China (in-sample R2 2.3-5.5%, OOS up to 4.5%) and Japan (R2 0.6-1.5%); weak in South Korea; ABSENT in Hong Kong and Singapore (OOS R2 near zero or negative). Effect weakened during COVID period in markets that have it. US replication of the original: R2 1.7% in-sample / 1.7% OOS for r1 alone, matching Gao. Source: published PDF (Monash repository), Tables 1-3.
- 数据需求：Same as Gao et al. (half-hour bars, 9-20 years).
- 局限：Shows the effect is market-structure-dependent, not a universal law - directly relevant caution before assuming it holds in 2024-2026 US single stocks. Volatility (not volume) is the binding conditioner.

### arXiv:2605.04004 (2026), Structural Limits of OHLCV-Based Intraday Signals in MNQ Futures: A Systematic Falsification Study (preprint, not peer-reviewed)
- 方法：Tests 14 intraday signal families (including opening-range/gap continuation and intraday momentum) on 947 trading days of 5-minute MNQ (Micro E-mini Nasdaq) bars, 2021-2025, with pre-registered deployment gates: OOS validation, t >= 2.0, >= 30 trades, positive net return after 2-point friction, yearly consistency.
- 关键结果：ZERO of 14 strategy families passed all gates. Gross edges were 0.07-1.50 points/trade versus a 2-point friction bar. Gap-continuation short showed t = 3.23, +14.52 pts/trade but on only 22 trades (failed sample-size gate). Positive controls confirmed the pipeline could detect real structure (T = 5.83 and 5.15). Source: arXiv abstract/PDF 2605.04004.
- 数据需求：947 days of 5-min bars - note this is nearly double our 5-min history and comparable to our hourly history, and it was still insufficient for any signal to clear honest gates.
- 局限：Single instrument, futures frictions larger than megacap-equity spreads; preprint. But it is the closest published analogue to what we would attempt with ~500-950 days of bar data, and its negative result is the base rate to beat.

### Wood, McInish, Ord (1985), An Investigation of Transactions Data for NYSE Stocks, Journal of Finance 40(3), 723-739
- 方法：Minute-by-minute market return series built from NYSE transaction records (Sep 1971 - Feb 1972 and full-year 1982); documents the intraday distribution of returns and variance by time of day.
- 关键结果：First systematic documentation of the intraday U-shape: returns and return variance are markedly elevated in the first 30 minutes after the open and in the final minutes before the close, with a quiet midday trough. This diurnal pattern has been confirmed in essentially every subsequent intraday dataset for four decades. Source: JF 1985 (venue/finding verified via citation trail; exact table values not re-extracted).
- 数据需求：Any intraday bars. Our 730 days x 7 hourly bars (3,535 bars/symbol; 35 symbols) estimate each symbol's hourly variance share with high precision; the 60-day 5-min data resolves the within-first-hour shape.
- 局限：The U-shape is a variance fact, not a return-predictability fact; it earns nothing by itself. Its value here is as the normalization curve (deseasonalizing hourly RV) and for scheduling: decisions made at 10:30 vs 15:30 face 2-3x different instantaneous vol.

### Andersen, Bollerslev (1997), Intraday Periodicity and Volatility Persistence in Financial Markets, Journal of Empirical Finance 4(2-3), 115-158; and Andersen, Bollerslev (1998), Answering the Skeptics, International Economic Review 39(4), 885-905
- 方法：1997: shows the diurnal U-shape must be filtered out (flexible Fourier form) before intraday GARCH dynamics are interpretable; 1998: evaluates daily GARCH(1,1) forecasts against realized volatility (RV) constructed from intraday returns instead of against noisy daily squared returns.
- 关键结果：1998 paper, DM-$ and Yen-$: R2 of GARCH(1,1) daily variance forecasts is 0.047 / 0.026 against daily squared returns, but rises to 0.383 / 0.419 when the target is RV built from HOURLY returns, and toward ~0.5 with 5-minute RV (their Table 4 progression). I.e., hourly bars already recover most of the volatility-forecasting information content of high-frequency data. Source: IER 1998 PDF (faculty.washington.edu mirror), pp. 891 and Table 4 discussion.
- 数据需求：Hourly bars suffice: daily RV from 7 hourly returns has relative sampling error ~sqrt(2/7) = 53% per day, which EWMA/HAR smoothing over 5-20 days reduces to usable precision. 730 days is ample: vol-persistence regressions on 500 daily RV observations produce t-statistics > 10 (persistence R2 30-60% is typical for HAR-type models, e.g., Corsi 2009, J. Financial Econometrics 7(2), 174-196).
- 局限：FX data in the original; equity RV persistence is if anything stronger. This is the one literature branch where our 730-day hourly set has overwhelming statistical power.

### Moreira, Muir (2017), Volatility-Managed Portfolios, Journal of Finance 72(4), 1611-1644; caveat: Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang, Yan (2020), On the Performance of Volatility-Managed Portfolios, Journal of Financial Economics 138(1), 95-117
- 方法：Scale exposure to a factor by c/RV(t-1) (inverse of trailing realized variance, typically previous-month daily RV): reduce exposure when recent vol is high. Alphas from spanning regressions of the managed on the unmanaged factor.
- 关键结果：Moreira-Muir: volatility management produces significantly positive alphas and higher Sharpe ratios for the market factor and momentum (market: alpha ~4.9%/yr in their Table I, Sharpe improvement roughly 0.42 to 0.5+), and large mean-variance utility gains. Cederburg et al. (103 factors, real-time implementable versions): managed portfolios do NOT systematically beat unmanaged ones out of sample; the market factor is among the FEW where the result survives real-time implementation. Sources: JF 2017 abstract/NBER w22208; JFE 2020 abstract (ScienceDirect S0304405X2030132X).
- 数据需求：Only daily returns strictly needed (decades available in our frozen data); hourly bars UPGRADE the vol estimate (previous entry: hourly RV recovers R2 0.38-0.42 vs 0.5 for 5-min), letting the regime layer react within 1-3 days instead of ~2 weeks.
- 局限：Cederburg et al. is a first-order caveat: gains are fragile for most factors, robust mainly for broad-market timing. For a long-only tech-megacap book, vol-managed scaling mainly buys drawdown control (helps the MDD <= 20% constraint), not the 30%/yr return target.

### Heston, Korajczyk, Sadka (2010), Intraday Patterns in the Cross-Section of Stock Returns, Journal of Finance 65(4), 1369-1407
- 方法：Cross-sectional regressions of half-hour stock returns on their own lagged half-hour returns at lags 1-520 (40 trading days x 13 half-hours), NYSE stocks 2001-2005.
- 关键结果：Striking return CONTINUATION spikes at lags that are exact multiples of 13 half-hours (i.e., same half-hour of day), persisting at least 40 trading days; volume, order imbalance, volatility, spreads show the same periodicity but do not explain the return pattern. Source: JF 2010 (SSRN 1107590 / arXiv 1005.3535 abstract).
- 数据需求：Large cross-section (thousands of stocks) of half-hour bars; the effect is a cross-sectional relative-performance pattern.
- 局限：With only 18 highly correlated tech stocks, the cross-sectional dispersion this effect trades on is mostly gone; and Yahoo hourly bars misalign with the half-hour grid. Treat as execution-timing knowledge (a stock that habitually rallies into the close tends to repeat), not as a strategy candidate for our universe.

### Lou, Polk, Skouras (2019), A Tug of War: Overnight Versus Intraday Expected Returns, Journal of Financial Economics 134(1), 192-213
- 方法：Decompose close-to-close returns into overnight (close-to-open) and intraday (open-to-close) using daily open prices; examine where anomaly/strategy profits accrue and the persistence of each component.
- 关键结果：Across 14 standard strategies, profits accrue either entirely overnight (momentum and reversal families) or entirely intraday, with opposite-signed offsets in the other period. Example: hedge portfolio sorted on past one-month INTRADAY returns earns 2.19%/month intraday (t = 6.72) with an offsetting overnight loss. Firm-level overnight and intraday return components each persist for years. Source: JFE 2019 (LSE mirror personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf; HKUST/RePEc listing).
- 数据需求：Only daily OHLC with reliable OPEN prices - our decades-long Yahoo daily data supports this at full power (e.g., a 4 bps/day overnight mean with 1.1% daily SD over 25 years gives t = 2.9; component-sorted strategies get far larger t-stats). Must first verify Yahoo's adjusted OPEN is adjusted with the same factors as close (check across split days: TSLA 2020-08-31, NVDA 2024-06-10).
- 局限：Cross-sectional long-short in the original; long-only adaptation keeps only the long leg. Overnight-capture trading doubles trade count (buy close, sell open) - zero-cost assumption hides ~2-6 bps/round-trip real spread cost on megacaps, which consumes a large fraction of a 3-5 bps/night edge; flag now to avoid a false-positive at the signal stage.

### Cooper, Cliff, Gulen (2008), Return Differences Between Trading and Non-Trading Hours: Like Night and Day, SSRN working paper 1004081 (unpublished in a journal)
- 方法：Split US equity/index/futures returns 1993-2006 into night (close-to-open) and day (open-to-close) components; compare means.
- 关键结果：The entire US equity premium 1993-2006 was earned overnight: for S&P 500 stocks, average night returns +2.82 to +4.76 bps/day versus day returns -2.85 to +0.22 bps/day; holds for indices, futures, NYSE and Nasdaq. Kelly-Clark (2011) confirm on ETFs 1999-2006. Source: SSRN 1004081 abstract and citing summaries.
- 数据需求：Daily open + close only - decades available in our frozen Yahoo data.
- 局限：Never journal-published; the night premium concentrates in high-beta/high-attention names (Berkman, Koch, Tuttle, Zhang 2012, J. Financial Markets 15(4), 571-598: attention-grabbing stocks open rich and reverse intraday - the OPPOSITE trade at the open). Funds built on the night effect (e.g., NightShares ETFs, closed 2023) died on costs - real-world confirmation that the gross edge is spread-sized.

**对本项目的适用性**：VERDICT ON 730 DAYS OF HOURLY BARS (~505 trading days x 7 bars = ~3,535 bars/symbol, 35 symbols). Power arithmetic (computed this session, standard OLS t approximation): a once-per-day predictive signal gives N=505; the minimum in-sample R2 detectable with 80% power at alpha=5% is 1.5% - which equals Gao et al.'s FULL published 1993-2013 effect. At plausible post-decay effect sizes the power collapses: R2=1.0% -> 62%, R2=0.5% -> 36%, R2=0.25% -> 20%. Pooling 35 tickers helps little (average pairwise correlation ~0.5-0.7 in this tech-heavy universe cuts 35 names to roughly 2x effective sample). CONCLUSION: 730 days is enough to CONFIRM a pre-registered, literature-anchored, sign-fixed effect at full published strength, but NOT enough to discover or tune hourly return signals - any parameter search across 35 symbols will produce false positives faster than true ones (the 2026 MNQ falsification study got zero survivors from 947 days of 5-min data). The 60-day 5-min and 30-day 1-min sets contain ~40-60 observations of any once-per-day event - validation-useless; use them only to verify hourly-bar timestamp conventions (S5) and the within-hour diurnal shape. RECOMMENDED USE, in descending feasibility: (1) HIGHEST POWER - volatility regime layer: hourly realized variance (deseasonalized by the U-shape curve) -> EWMA/HAR daily RV forecast -> long/flat or vol-target scaling of the existing low-frequency signal layer. Vol persistence gives t>10 with 500 days; hourly RV recovers R2 0.38-0.42 of the information vs ~0.5 for 5-min (Andersen-Bollerslev 1998), and Moreira-Muir-style market-level scaling is the one variant that survived the Cederburg critique. This directly serves the MDD<=20% constraint; it will not by itself deliver 30%/yr. (2) FEASIBLE AS A SINGLE PRE-REGISTERED CONFIRMATION TEST - Baltussen-style intraday momentum: rLH (15:30-16:00, which is exactly Yahoo's last hourly bar) regressed on rROD (prior close to 15:30), long-only version on QQQ/SPY, sign and approximate magnitude frozen from the 2021 JFE paper, one test, no search. Even if confirmed, expected contribution is single-digit %/yr. (3) DO ON DAILY DATA INSTEAD - overnight/intraday decomposition (gap continuation/reversal, night premium): needs only open+close, so run it on the DECADES of daily history at full power; do not spend the hourly budget on it. Verify Yahoo adjusted-open consistency on known split dates first, and treat the doubled trade count as a cost landmine before promoting any overnight-capture result. (4) SKIP - hourly TSMOM on single stocks: no literature support (Baltussen is futures close-vs-day structure, not hourly autocorrelation); with 3,535 bars the minimum detectable |AC1| at 80% power is 4.7% while realistic hourly equity autocorrelation is under 2% and slightly negative. Also skip Heston-Korajczyk-Sadka same-half-hour seasonality as a strategy (needs thousands of stocks; our 18-name cross-section is too correlated).
